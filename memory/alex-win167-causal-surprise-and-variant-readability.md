# WIN 167: causal surprise and readable evolution

Recorded September 22, 2026 from Alex's explicit feedback on **WIN sep 22**. These are reusable editorial decisions and their rationale, not a claim that a particular concept is approved or improves retention. [Revision record](reels/win167-revision-record.md) · [source snapshot and verification status](../video/src/win167/README.md).

## Diagnose the idea before adding effects

Alex repeatedly rejected birds, wings, website/phone mockups, buildings, a robot, a wrecking ball and a mirror because the action was boring or its relationship to Darwin/evolution was unclear. This is feedback about those executions in WIN, not a universal ban on those objects. The BOSS reel was a reference for a large authority figure, conflict, anticipation and consequence; copying its fight does not automatically explain evolution.

**Show the narrated relationship:** alternatives attempt the same task, one visibly wins, the retained winner produces descendants, and those descendants retain something useful while changing. Ask what a muted viewer can identify as the task, the winner, the inherited trait and the new trait. If any answer depends on a paragraph of labels, revise the action or staging first. More motion, particles or unrelated spectacle will not resolve an unclear relationship.

## Preferences to carry forward

- **A reveal needs a next event.** The latest 3s note rejects candidates that pop out and then bounce in place. After arrival, make the protagonists operate, collide, struggle, transform or cause a handoff that changes the state. Review the entire local shot, especially the gap after the entrance. A camera push, extra sparks and idle bounces do not supply a second event.
- **Surprise must remain causal.** Establish a readable expectation, interrupt it, then make the result understandable. The accepted hook direction combined book pressure, mutation glimpses and a larger breakthrough. Do not generalize its exact book/color sequence into a template for every scene. Faster movement alone does not make a predictable action surprising.
- **Hierarchy belongs to the subject.** Alex asked for a large wise Darwin with a conspicuous white beard and later a larger Claude before the crush. Keep the canonical Claude anatomy and expressive face, stage visible contact, compress the body against planted feet, and make the release a consequence of the compression. Extra background activity cannot rescue a small or inert protagonist.
- **Lineage and variation need different visual signals.** Carry one invariant from the winner into its descendants, such as the spring boots, then give each descendant a distinct body color and clearly different equipment silhouette/action. The earlier all-green solution helped ancestry but failed the latest glance-readability request. Alex explicitly asks for different colors or strong visual differences near 18s; this overrides the earlier all-green implementation. Show the same mapping in the following trial rather than recoloring characters at the cut.
- **Effects should connect cause to target.** Alex requested a lightning zap when Darwin swings his feather around 15s. Follow the actual feather tip to the intended target, synchronize target recoil and visible change, then clear the effect from faces. The later request for less predictable animation shows that this successful local mechanism is not a substitute for a stronger scene concept. Do not attach lightning to every beat by default.
- **Represent a cost as a loss.** “Burns through tokens fast” needs a visible reserve decreasing as work consumes it. Coins flowing into a machine that only makes Claude larger can read as a power benefit. Show input leaving a finite stock and being spent/burned, with the remaining stock visibly lower by the end. A label saying “tokens” cannot repair the wrong visual direction.
- **An ending must do new work.** The 23s ascending-stage recap was rejected. “Build a draft, then evolve it” needs an operated transformation with a legible before and after, not a repeat of already-seen character poses. A cocoon/metamorphosis scene is the implemented local-preview candidate, not a standing preference or an approved solution.
- **Annotations direct attention while preserving the evidence.** Alex explicitly requested Boris's real stage footage from BOSS, large and sharp; then an arrow pointing at him and a Claude logo at top-right on “this is the future of AI”; then a more active bouncing arrow. Keep the arrow's tip associated with Boris throughout motion and keep his face clear. This is a scoped, explicit exception to the [no filmed B-roll default](alex-faceless-no-live-action-broll.md), not permission to introduce unrelated stock footage.
- **Varied sound follows varied physical events.** Differentiate compression, fracture, spring release, selection, energy contact, equipment and resource consumption by their material/action sounds. Accent the payoff without obscuring speech. Check cue onset against the actual contact, inspect the isolated stem and listen in the full mix. More cue count or more gain alone does not prove stimulation; recent preview cue densities are historical measurements, not new defaults overriding [sound design](../docs/SOUND-DESIGN.md).
- **Background contrast must help the current action.** Alex rejected plain repeating backgrounds and requested stronger contrast. Change set, palette and depth when the story changes; keep the moving hero distinct from the immediate backdrop. Background variety must not break continuity of the winner or compete with the main event.
- **The cover has its own design system.** Alex explicitly asked to fix the existing cover and remember the system for future covers. Use [cover-system](../cover-system/README.md), its shared `SceneCover`, locked typography, quiet zone, crop safety and native/150px verification. A reel frame or hook header is not a substitute for a composed cover.

## Verification and scope

Use dense temporal samples and normal-speed playback, not just peak-action stills. Review the named timestamps plus their lead-in and follow-through at native resolution and phone scale. Confirm contact, inherited identity, distinct alternatives, effect emitters/targets, arrow placement and resource depletion. Preserve the original recorded voice/caption timeline unless Alex specifically requests a timing change.

Independent visual reviews and technical gates caught genuine defects, yet Alex continued rejecting the concepts. **A technical pass is not creative approval.** The explicit “great now let me see full video” accepted the then-current continuous-motion hook sufficiently to expand the preview; it did not approve every subsequent body scene. Later full previews remain subject to the latest feedback. “People will scroll away” is Alex's creative concern, not reported retention data.
