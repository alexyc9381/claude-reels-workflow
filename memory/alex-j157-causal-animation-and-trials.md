## Reusable prevention rules

1. **Represent the narrated verb with an event that changes the situation.** Before drawing, write subject → anticipation → operation/contact → result → handoff. A dial, numeric counter, generic shield or labelled phone is not automatically an interesting representation. When several timestamps are called boring, revise the scene ideas throughout, not only easing, decoration or cue count. Inspect the hook-to-body handoff separately.
2. **Use a different meaningful mechanism for each claim.** Decision throughput can become discrete commands completing a circuit; speed can become a race; lower cost can become a physical cut and refund. These are examples tied to this voice, not a new circuit/race/scissors template for every reel. Keep one main event and let secondary detail support it.
3. **Give the comparison a readable center and honest scope.** Fix the ratio and action as a composition, not only by centering a text box. Reserve space above the moving subject; inspect the whole path for overlap. A miniature race illustrates speed, not measured race footage or invented model rankings. Preserve the actual source ratio, here 20–200× and 40–400×, rather than changing facts to match an ambiguous spoken feedback transcription.
4. **Real faces can receive purposeful emphasis without being replaced.** Alex explicitly requested a ring travelling around each circular portrait as it enters. Trace the circumference, keep the face clear, time the ring to that portrait and let it resolve. Repeating unrelated pulses is not the requested effect. Source real headshots, demo, official art and the actual article when those are the subject.
5. **More robot detail must include articulation and work.** Design recognizable joints, limbs, feet, grip and tool, plus a furnished room with depth. Show a specific mess → cleaning action → changed floor. Keep limb joints attached while stepping, a planted foot grounded, and the hand/nozzle/hose in compatible coordinates. More decorative panels on the same static silhouette do not fix the request.
6. **A voice interaction needs a response the viewer can see.** Alex's example was ORDER A PIZZA followed by pizzas filling the screen. Build request → listening → confirmation → staggered arrivals → character reaction. Richness can come from escalating quantities and opening boxes; preserve the main subject and caption hierarchy. Do not synthesize new narration merely because a visual request contains quoted dialogue.
7. **Finish the reaction and payoff.** The cup rescue needs a catch, recovery and safe return, not a suspended cup. Archery needs aim, release, intercept and a visible result, not a stationary target replacing a shield. Check meaningful travel, contact, follow-through and exit at normal playback speed as well as key frames.
8. **Keep the ending active.** Alex predicted a drop near 30s and requested a stamp landing on the article. Stage a visible downstroke, real contact, paper response, rebound and persistent ink result, with impact sound at contact. This is a user concern, not supplied analytics. Preserve the actual closing keyword timing; J-157 COMMENT/J times must not replace other reels' CTA schedules.
9. **Claude performs a role.** Directed gaze, anticipation, exertion, surprise, relief and celebration follow the event. Maintain original sprite identity and rig constraints. Do not use idle bouncing or a camera push as the main action. Inspect face clearance and safe bounds during maximum movement, not just the settled pose.
10. **Sound changes with the action.** Re-time cues after changing a mechanism, duration or cut. Give distinct hooks separate effect schedules and verify each full encoded mix. A timestamp with mixed-audio energy does not prove the specific effect is audible. Listen to stems and the full mix; do not promote this reel's cue counts or gains into universal targets.

## Iteration must catch actual defects

The first rebuild preview exposed clipped Claude during the cup dive, race text crowding the driver, a receipt masking rectangle, target discontinuity at arrow contact and a sparse pizza payoff. Later passes corrected grip/world coordinates, cup return and the medal result. Generalize the inspections, not just the final prop choices:

- Inspect start, anticipation, maximum travel, contact, recovery and tail for every changed operation; add adjacent frames around impacts. Whole-reel contact sheets reveal repetition, but cannot prove smooth timing or audible synchronization.
- Anchor contacting objects in a common coordinate system or explicitly transform between spaces. Check saucer/cup, hand/tool, arrow/target and stamp/paper together.
- Keep movement continuous across phase changes; do not teleport the target at impact or hide a receipt with an obviously mismatched rectangle.
- Review sequence density and foreground occupancy at phone size. Eight staggered pizzas solved one scene's sparse payoff; eight is not a global requirement.
- Rewrite storyboard and package the **last** source after the last visual/audio change. Do not ship an old ZIP alongside a new MP4.

## Gates, review limits and delivery

The delivered revision had nine audio/caption checks and gated look checks passing on each cut, but **motion failures remained**: cup catch and robot on main/Dodge, plus Cartridge's hook. Whole-reel motion medians remained below target. Disclosure was necessary; it did not fix those failures or grant permission to bypass future gates. Treat this as unfinished quality debt in the historical delivery, not an approved low-motion precedent. Preserve established thresholds; improve the action rather than adding unrelated background traffic to game them.
