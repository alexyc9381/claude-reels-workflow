# SETUP: detailed 2D scenes and purposeful performance

Source: Alex's September 9–10, 2026 X/SETUP production chat. This note captures the
follow-ups after the [earlier concept, retention and sound feedback](alex-abstract-animation-and-audible-sfx.md).
Load both when revising this reel or applying its lessons to a future brief.

## Explicit preferences from the later messages

- Stay with the established **2D** treatment for this SETUP revision. Alex disliked the
  3D aspect and requested better 2D hooks based on the repository system. This is not a
  universal ban on 3D in unrelated projects.
- Remove “start by losing the wasted input” and “More plugins ≠ more useful.” Do not
  replace deleted filler with another explanatory slogan. Keep the useful outcome header
  and narration captions; remove duplicate copy when the action already communicates it.
  The earlier ban on tiny bottom-left production notes still applies.
- Keep Claude identity visible throughout. Alex suggested a **low-opacity rotating Claude
  logo** in the background. Treat that as subordinate identity, not the main animation.
  Use authentic product logos with their colors and proportions intact.
- “Top 1% Claude user” needs a visually compelling payoff, not the rejected chatbox.
  A crown/laurel rank-up was implemented: anticipation, arrival, landing response and
  expression change. That implementation is an example, not a universal crown template
  or proof of user approval.
- Give each opening key/tool its own interesting activation: Alex suggested glow and
  jingling. Use a distinct contact, brief glow, damped swing and matched material sound;
  keep the three activations readable and preserve the quick opening zoom.
- Vary backgrounds across the reel. Change architecture, depth, lighting and composition
  where the subject changes, not just a hue over the same empty backdrop.
- Make **each scene** more detailed, professional and actively animated. The later
  request specifically named **X as the visual-detail reference**.

## Resolve the X reference correctly

Alex previously said X did not retain viewers and its animations lost interest after the
hook. Later, he asked for SETUP's detail and polish to resemble X. These refer to different
qualities. **X is explicitly permitted as a reference for visual detail; it is not a proven
retention or pacing template.** The older index wording “X is not an approved creative
reference” was too broad after this clarification.

Inspect the reference's exported frames for the requested qualities. In this revision,
that meant layered rooms, practical lamps, deep shadows, foreground fixtures, differentiated
materials and the original Claude rig's supported costumes. Preserve the independent
SETUP staging boundary: do not revive a rejected SETUP concept just because a new shader,
logo, color or decorative layer makes it look different.

## Turn detail into readable action

A detailed setting alone does not repair a boring event. Pair the craft pass with a
performance pass on the primary mechanism and Claude:

1. Name the narrated verb, its cause and its changed result. Review the 3–7-second
   hook-to-body handoff independently; the first tool must begin doing useful work.
2. Add detail that explains construction or operation: threads, joints, fasteners, lens
   rims, bindings, controls, source paths or working surfaces. Keep it subordinate to the
   main silhouette. Plain rectangles/circles plus labels were explicitly rejected.
3. Drive secondary motion from the operation: coupled gears follow the press; locking
   bolts retract after the key turns; source evidence travels into the report. Do not add
   perpetual background movement to inflate a motion score.
4. Give the original Claude rig a job and causal expression changes. Preserve anatomy;
   use supported costumes when appropriate. Keep costumes stable within a scene: staggered
   blink clocks must not accidentally change a character's role at a scene boundary.
5. Inspect at phone size and through the full action, including its ending. Check face,
   hand/prop contact, label and logo clearance. The detailed HUD pass exposed gears crossing
   the context label; moving the mechanism behind the dial restored legibility.
6. Retain the narration, captions and selected music when the feedback concerns visuals.
   Keep action-matched SFX audible throughout without sounding every small movement.

Implementation examples in the detailed SETUP pass: a driven compression press, a linked
HUD instrument, book/edit/search work with evidence transfers, a key-operated access vault,
walking traffic into a competitor storefront, evidence assembling into a bound report,
and the three-tool keyring above a dispatch case. These are case examples, not a mandatory
set of props to repeat in future reels. Similarweb/Semrush must still communicate the
value of traffic/search evidence becoming useful research, not merely display their logos.

## Verification and approval are separate

The delivered detailed pass had all eight scenes above the per-scene motion floor, no
scene-tail stalls, a passing look audit and 9/9 export checks. Its reported motion median
was **7.86 versus the historical 9.00 target**, so it was not a complete motion-benchmark
pass. Its decoded audio was sample-identical to the prior verified delivery. These are
case measurements, not targets or retention evidence.

Alex's requests to upload the reel or push these learnings are not an explicit creative
approval or audience-performance result. No measured retention curve was supplied here.
