# ⛔⛔⛔ A CHANGE OF FRAMING IS A CUT WHETHER OR NOT YOU AUTHOR ONE

Alex, on reel 119: *"why are there two cuts here for this ox thing at the beginning? needs
to be one smooth animation. **PREVENT THIS IN THE FUTURE.**"*
He counted two. I had authored one.

| what he saw | what it was |
|---|---|
| cut 1 | a real hard cut, CLOSE on the gate -> WIDE of the room |
| cut 2 | the full-frame steel clearing four frames later — **the whole frame changes content, which reads as an edit even though no cut exists** |

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE RULE

**A cut is only earned when it REVEALS something a continuous take cannot.**
A change of framing is a cut. A full-frame mass leaving is a change of framing.
If one camera position can carry both moments, **use one**.

⛔ This is a correction to how AGENCY's three-cuts-in-2.5s structure was being applied.
That structure is right and it is measured (reel 78: 2.0 for one wide -> 6.23 for three
shots with no new material) — **but the lift comes from each cut REVEALING something.**
On 119, cut 1 (close on steel -> a whole room) was real; cut 2 showed the same room from
the same angle a beat later, so it bought nothing and cost continuity.

## ⭐⭐ THE MOVE THAT MAKES ONE FRAMING POSSIBLE: MAKE THE BARRIER SEE-THROUGH

All three of 119's hooks were close-then-wide. All three became single 75-frame takes by
changing what the barrier is:
- the gate became **BARS** instead of a solid leaf
- the paywall got a **HOLE** in it
- the price board was never full-frame to begin with

Now the room and the subject are visible from frame 0, and the lift/detonation changes the
**access**, not the subject. Nothing is revealed by cutting, so there is nothing to cut to.
⭐ It also answered a separate note — *"the OX should already be visible coming in at 0
seconds"* — in the same edit.

**Cost of the rebuild: nothing.** Hook motion 15.57 / 14.05 / 15.31, medians 12.83 /
14.05 / 14.36, 0/11 scenes under bar on every cut.

## ⛔ AND IF YOU DO CUT BETWEEN TWO VIEWS OF THE SAME SUBJECT, IT MUST MATCH

Earlier on the same reel: *"it glitches at 1 second showing small oxen then they snap and
get big."* Shot B ended on two oxen at s=0.60/1.04; shot C opened on one at 1.62. Both
shots were correct in isolation. With no camera change between them the viewer does not
read "new shot", they read **the same animals snapping bigger** — indistinguishable from a
bug. Make the outgoing shot END at exactly the size and x the incoming shot BEGINS at, and
do not have a subject that exists in only one of the two.

⛔ **Re-time every cue when you merge shots.** Merging moved the iron 4 frames and a tag
cascade 8; the cue list does not follow the picture on its own.
