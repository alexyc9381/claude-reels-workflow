## 1. The hook needs a consequence after the hit

The requests were a bigger snapping glove, stronger disappearance, a darkened room, glowing Claude with exaggerated eyes, an opening zoom, and repeated improvements at 2–3 seconds. A powerful first hit followed by a held pose still failed.

- Begin the subject's action immediately. A small camera push supplements it.
- Stage anticipation, contact, transformation and the next action; inspect the full first three seconds, not only frame zero.
- Do not let an entrance's clamped final value freeze the subject through the cut. Give the next action its own timing driver.
- ECC's implemented sequence: lift → snap → workstations disintegrate → Claude powers up → gauntlet winds up and thrusts → specialists materialize → staggered forward launches into the team scene.

The glove, dark-room transformation and godlike eyes are this reel's requested treatment. They are not a universal hook recipe or evidence of virality.

## 2. Background sprites have jobs too

Alex explicitly said the sprites at 4 seconds must be moving **with actions**, then asked to find similar opportunities across the reel. This qualifies older rules that literally freeze every secondary subject: preserve one clear primary action, but do not use hierarchy to justify a lifeless workforce.

Give each supporting actor a task, object, destination, gaze and reaction. Stagger their timing. Keep their scale and contrast below the foreground event. Typing has alternating hands and changing monitor content; inspection moves a lens over a blueprint; a handoff has a sender, travelling tool and receiver. Random nodding, synchronized bouncing and camera drift are not substitutes.

The delivered background uses three workshop tiers and 21 slightly enlarged workers. That count is an ECC implementation choice, not a minimum crowd size. Review the whole panel at native resolution and phone scale so small hands, tools and expressions remain readable without obscuring the skill-case opening.

## 3. More motion must still have a beginning and ending

Alex rejected the 30-second conductor because the characters were "just bouncing back and forth" and asked for a beginning and end. A loop can depict ongoing work, but the scene must change state and finish its event.

Define the entry state, operation, result and handoff before adding detail. ECC's replacement is three specialists feeding design/code/test pages into one stack, closing the ECC cover and presenting the finished repository. The earlier skill case is not reused for this or the later reveal. Changing color, logos or costume does not make a repeated action new.

When a user expands the request to the rest of the video, inspect the whole sequence. The later pass added a star toss into the collector, real tool grips for the designers, a second specialist capturing and wheeling away the security bug, and an operated crank/rollers during testing. These changes give existing characters a role in the outcome.

## 4. Operate graphics; do not park logos or unreadable screenshots

The rejected beats included the zoomed-out GitHub page near 8 seconds, low-motion logos at 10–13 seconds, a basic skills clock, and another repository screenshot near the ending.

Use recognizable product marks at readable sizes when identity is intended. Keep their aspect ratio. Prefer an actual action around the mark: tap a phone, load a cartridge, pull a lever, type and submit a prompt, catch an output. A drawing remains an illustration, not proof that an installation ran.

ECC uses the actual Claude Code and Codex marks, a longer four-instruction prompt, and a dedicated prompt view that clears competing elements. The install sequence before it unfolds an editor, feeds an ECC cartridge, locks it and pulls a lever.

Alex asked for real creator footage if possible. Retrieval failed; the implemented shot uses a genuine photograph from the official repository. Do not report that as video footage or invent a talking-head substitute.

Alex specifically requested removing "ECC" around 8 seconds because viewers might leave to search. Replacing the badge alone would leave the name in the expanded title and anticipatory karaoke caption.

For that request, inspect the prop, headline, subtitles including their lead-in, visible URL and screenshot content. The delivered 7.85–9.7s scene has an operated phone, a name-free `ONE POWERFUL REPO / FREE ON GITHUB` header, and three dots in place of the early displayed ECC word. The original aligned transcript and spoken audio are unchanged. Later scenes and the final ECC CTA retain their names.

This is an explicit early-visual-reveal exception to older advice to always name the artifact at introduction. Do not silently bleep narration, suppress every later name, or make all future videos anonymous. Preserve the requested reveal timing and audit all surfaces that could defeat it.
