## Rules for subsequent reels

1. Inspect 2–3 seconds before a reported loss point, not only the frame at the timestamp. Viewers may decide to leave before the graph records the loss.
2. Give every tutorial transition an immediate useful action and a visible consequence. Avoid blank composer waits, passive PDF holds, and several consecutive shots of the same window with different labels.
3. Storyboard each beat as **starting state → actor action → changed result → next reason to watch**. A moving card or a zoom is not a substitute for a result.
4. Specify the hero's last meaningful action and inspect the remaining time. Check settled entrances and camera movement separately; whole-scene averages can conceal a local lull.
5. Show the comment keyword at the beginning of the CTA. Keep the promised artifact doing something useful through the final phrase, with only a short readable settle at the end.
6. Match expressive Claude motion to a task: load, press, inspect, react, deliver. Preserve gaze, anticipation, contact, and recovery; do not add perpetual bobbing to inflate motion measurements.
7. Review native frames at entry, mid-action, contact, peak scale, and exit. Protect the keyword, face, paper title, and captions through the entire movement, not only at rest.
8. Retiming animation also requires retiming its audio. Check actual SFX stems, not just energy in a mix containing speech and music. Preserve the original narration and approved music unless there is a separate reason to recut them.
9. Keep numerical motion/look/audio gates, but add a semantic review: can a muted viewer describe what changed? Can the CTA be read before the expected exit? Never call a metric pass “engaging” or claim recovered retention from an export.
