## 1. The hook needs a second event, not a label for the spoken line

**Feedback:** the initial hook was uninteresting; use a zipper or door and make the Claude specialists spill out. The zipper looked glitched, nothing happened afterward, the FREE label merely echoed the voice, and the action near two seconds remained too slow. Later: more shaking in the intro.

**Why:** an opening can promise a reveal and still run out of action immediately after it. Repeating “free” supplies no new visual consequence. More movement on the same finished pose cannot repair that information gap.

**Do:** connect opening → spill → operated tool → output → reaction/handoff. JOB cuts from the zipper at 1.42s to a writer jumping onto a mechanical keyboard, landing around 1.9s and launching a draft around 2s. The commenter catches/passes a reply while the writer chases the departing draft. Distinct outfits/colors make the eleven specialists readable as roles.

- Give the subject a real action from frame zero; the slight opening zoom only supports it.
- Keep zipper teeth, cloth edges, slider and attached operator driven by shared geometry. More shake must not conceal a broken zipper.
- Use short, damped shakes at actual pull/landing/stomp contacts. Keep the canonical header readable; avoid a perpetual random shake.
- Inspect 0–1s, 1–3s **and the tail into the next scene**. R8's first draft had strong early motion but a weak tail; the actual fix was a chase/reply handoff, not background noise or a lower gate.

## 2. Match the narrated operation; don't dress the words in shapes

**Feedback:** typing at ~7s/~13s was boring; “proven algorithms” needed graphics. “Super robotic” needed robot performance, “humanizer” needed a transformation, and the third quarter felt like text, rectangles, radar, magnifying glasses and basic back-and-forth movement.

**Why:** a label tells viewers the topic; an operation shows what changes. A paper, lens, radar or robot is not automatically interesting merely because it is a graphic. Repeated symbolic containers can be as static as repeated text cards.

**Do:** write actor → anticipation → contact/operation → changed state → next consequence before building. Replace the full criticized span when several timestamps share the same defect. JOB's 24.50–38.67s rebuild uses separate jargon-bot press contacts, five detector bumpers reacting to a travelling draft, a giant robot reaching for publish, an officer's emergency stomp/gate, human authorization and an approval stamp. Humanizer peels away a rigid metal casing to reveal warm Claude.

These illustrate the words; they do not prove detector performance or constitute actual software operations. Preserve optional-API/result qualifiers. Do not turn these examples into “always use pinball/presses/books.”

## 3. Faster means shorter complete actions, not arbitrary snapping

**Feedback:** later animation was slow and basic, but movement also looked unnaturally snappy/random. The ~32s robot needed to be much bigger, more exaggerated and supported by sound.

**Why:** speed and continuity are separate. Uniform acceleration, repeated left-right travel or large idle bobbing can increase pixel motion without conveying effort, weight or a result.

**Do:** retain anticipation, a readable contact, recoil and recovery at action-specific durations. Enlarge the dominant subject before adding decorative motion. Claude's gaze, exertion, surprise and relief should follow the event; preserve the original rig, short limbs and recognizable proportions. Keep detailed backgrounds subordinate to the main operation.

Check the actual interaction: one press must not hit two targets when only one is meant to crush; a stamp must meet the seal/paper; a key must turn at a socket; the emergency action must stop the robot before publishing. JOB's self-review corrected all four kinds of staging. A stopped character still needs a consequence: the robot retreats from the blocked control rather than remaining frozen.

**Do:** keep recognizable post/comment/reply/profile structure, useful draft content, authentic marks and portrait images. JOB uses two sourced professional stock photos for fictional UI avatars. Keep source records and sensible crops; do not imply the stock subjects are real customers, account owners or endorsers. Keep the exact repository URL as a readable anchor. **This is an installation metaphor, not a literal interface or live demo.** For a tutorial whose purpose is teaching real clicks, preserve authentic controls and the actual workflow instead.

## 6. Sound must identify the action and be audible against the other stems

**Feedback:** effects were uninteresting/quiet; use chimes, actual robot sounds, stronger impacts and loud mechanical keyboard typing. Music should start at **8 seconds of Another Day Of Sun**, not near the end of the track.

**Why:** generic effects at scene cuts cannot describe a robot jam, a key strike or a gate arrest. Full-mix energy at a cue timestamp may be entirely narration/music. A later loud peak inside a multi-note phrase does not by itself prove an air swell, but that observation must not become an excuse to ignore named banned sounds.

**Do:** source from the user's AM Creator library, match sounds to operations, preserve natural tails, and re-time after animation changes. JOB adds actual robot chatter/jam, darker servo motors, metal contacts, low thocks and chimes. Mechanical typing uses distinct recorded excerpts with the requested click detail. A sustained scanner tone was removed because it overlapped the detector contacts.

- Measure actual SFX, voice and music stems separately; compare like windows. R7's SFX stem was +4.58 dB over R6 overall and +7.99 dB over 24.5–38.667s. Those are RMS differences, **not perceived-loudness, intelligibility or retention measurements**, and not universal gain targets.
- Distinguish **source offset** from **timeline onset**. Here source 8.000s plays at reel time zero. Independently decoding the original passage and comparing PCM verified the selection. The 8s value is JOB-specific and must not silently replace another reel's requested passage.
- Keep the raw audit. JOB's legacy spectral audit flags the specifically requested bright multi-stroke keyboard and multi-note robot chatter. The rationale and exceptions are disclosed; this is **not an all-audio-gates-clean claim or a global waiver**. Named air/whoosh bans remain.
- Listen to the source/stems/full mix when playback is available. This session used source/signal/contact checks; subjective listening and small-speaker playback were not performed. Do not imply otherwise.
