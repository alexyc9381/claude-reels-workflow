# SETUP: the first second must contain an event

Alex rejected the first delivered SETUP trial hooks: they did not have enough beginning
motion to entice continued viewing or provide a pattern interrupt. The displayed file was
trial B; his wording addressed the hook angles together, so both B and C were revised.
The previous technical passes are **not creative approval**.

## What the first-second review found

The previous hooks spent roughly their first second preparing to act. Their six-frame
camera push created an initial motion spike, followed by small prop travel and character
anticipation. Their useful tool actions started much later. A full-hook average hid that
weak opening interval.

The correction changes the event and its scale while keeping the existing camera timing:

- **B: immediate magnetic sweep.** A large magnet sweeps across the foreground in the first
  eight frames. Plugs rush toward it, its catch pulls Claude across the floor, and Claude
  plants his feet and reclaims control. Three useful tools then separate and earn the rank
  payoff on the existing spoken phrase.
- **C: immediate launch and relay.** An oversized packet launches at frame 4 (~0.13s),
  reaches compression at ~0.5s, then visibility and access. During “these three,” the working
  tools dock into a larger hero arrangement, followed by the existing top-1% payoff.
- Foley is retimed to sweep/catch/drag/plant and launch/compression/scan/unlock/dock contacts.
  The prepared narration and the body mix after frame 109 remain unchanged.

## Review rule for future hooks

1. Review frames 0, 4, 8, 13, 20 and 28 before approving the entire hook. The main subject
   must already be visible and a meaningful action must begin immediately.
2. A pattern interrupt needs a clear physical event with a consequence: a sweep catches
   something, a launch enters an operation, a load pulls the character, a changed object
   earns the next beat. It is not synonymous with camera shake, flash, random debris or
   a busier background.
3. Check the 0.25–1.0s interval separately. The opening camera move must not mask a return
   to near-still preparation once the zoom ends.
4. Use large, readable travel and changing silhouettes. Keep the face clear, preserve the
   original rig, and inspect peak positions for header/edge collisions. Purposeful cropping
   of an entering foreground prop does not authorize clipping Claude's face or key labels.
5. Follow the first hit with another useful event. Do not reveal everything in 300ms and
   leave a long hold until the narration catches up. Preserve word-aligned later payoffs.
6. Keep sound contacts distinct and voice intelligible. A louder impact cannot rescue an
   opening whose subject still stands waiting.

## Diagnostic evidence, not a retention prediction

At 30fps, panel-only mean grayscale change over the first second changed from 3.87 to
10.64 for B and 3.74 to 8.47 for C. In the 0.25–1.0s interval, it changed from 2.38 to
8.12 for B and 2.11 to 5.13 for C. Camera timing was unchanged. This describes increased
subject motion, **not a predicted engagement or retention multiplier**. The rendered
sequence and audience response remain the creative tests.
