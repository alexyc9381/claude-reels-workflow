## THE HOOK TOOK SIX PASSES AND FIVE OF THEM ARE REUSABLE

It opened at **5.02, the only STATIC scene in the reel**, and finished at **10.18**. The useful part
is that the first three fixes were all reasonable and all worth almost nothing.

| # | change | result | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | one rising `strain` → three failed hauls | 5.02 → **4.91** | correct diagnosis, wrong magnitude — see #4 |
| 2 | 54-frame light ramp → three discrete rank snaps | 4.91 → **4.96** | right idea, but the room was 80% static under it |
| 3 | web `fall` across the dead tail | 4.96 → **5.23** | the cords were dark-on-dark, see #6 |
| 4 | rake 0.10 → 0.16 @ 9.4, + a background process | 5.23 → **6.26** | ⭐ the hook had the LOWEST rake in the reel |
| 5 | **split into two framings on the VO's own break** | 6.26 → **7.0** | 157f in one framing; reel 104's winning open is 2.57s |
| 6 | hauls 44px → 150px, rig connected, drop beat moved inside its shot, shot B lit | → **10.18** | |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The four findings

1. **AN ACTION IS A DISTANCE, AND 12% IS NOT ONE.** The three hauls drove `0.42 × reach 104` = 44px
   on a 352px sprite. On a frame strip they were indistinguishable from each other and from the
   rest of the scene. At `0.95 × 158` = 150px (43% of the body) the strip reads three pulls. The
   fix was never more frames.
2. **EVERYTHING HE TOUCHES MOVES WITH HIM.** The moment the haul became real travel, the cord end,
   the forearm and the pliers all stayed where they were — he was hauling on nothing and holding a
   tool a body-width away. Every prop in a sprite's hands has to read the same `drive * reach`
   expression the sprite does.
3. **A TIMED BEAT AUTHORED PAST ITS OWN CUT NEVER HAPPENS.** `TURN`/`DROP` were 108/118 while shot A
   ends at 104, so the arrival the whole open builds to — he lets go — existed in the code and not
   in the video. Moving them to 86/92 also filled the one dead window the per-frame profile found
   (f83-98 at 3.35 against a scene mean of 7.0). §6.1, verbatim, again.
4. **THE SAME PROP, COPIED INTO A DARKER SET, STOPS EXISTING.** Shot A's cords are dark cable on a
   pale room and read perfectly. Copied into shot B's dark hall they became nine large objects
   sweeping ~15% of the panel per sample at a luma delta of nearly zero — invisible to a viewer and
   worth nothing to the audit, which is the same blindness twice. A `light` palette and ending the
   fall against the LIT field fixed both at once.

## ⛔⛔⛔ THE TANGLE: THREE WRONG SHAPES, AND THE THIRD MEANT THE OBJECT

The bench's pile of dead cable was drawn three times and read as something else every time:
**straight coloured sticks = pencils in a jar · even U-loops in a row = croquet hoops · overlapping
ellipses = Olympic rings.** All three failed for one reason: *a regular array of a simple shape
reads as whatever regular array the viewer already knows, never as a mess.*

⭐ `feedback_three_notes_means_the_object` called it: the first note is values, the second is
staging, the third means **the object is wrong**. A heap of slack cable is ambiguous at any quality.
What is not ambiguous — and what actually says "somebody has been wiring this by hand for hours" —
is **cords that are PLUGGED IN and CROSSING EACH OTHER**. That is the one image everybody has of a
manual exchange, and it is literally what a hand-built node graph looks like. `WebOfCords` replaced
the pile, and it became the scene's biggest motion source as well as its argument.

## GOTCHAS WORTH THE NEXT AGENT'S TIME

- ⛔ **A bare `<img src="logos/x.svg">` renders as a broken-image placeholder.** A relative src
  resolves against the BUNDLE url, not `public/`. Every mark tile in the first build was broken —
  visible in a still, invisible to every gate. Use Remotion's `<Img src={staticFile(...)}>`.
- ⛔ **A label row collided with the art THREE times in one reel** (5 over the hook's field, 6 over
  shot B's, 19 over S6's). Each time the answer was fewer and bigger, and at S6 the deeper answer
  was §4: the depiction is the banks lighting and the total climbing, and **text is not a depiction**.
- ⛔ **The frame-edge occluder is z 94 and plates were z 78-86**, so `19 CATEGORIES` rendered as
  `9 CATEGORIES`. Plates are chrome; they go above it.
- ⛔ **`sfx_audit` flagged five cues and four were real.** `machine_bed`/`shop_bed` are broadband
  hiss (peakiness 10.8 / 9.3 against a floor of 14) — replaced with `stage_hum` (254.6) and
  `engine_idle` (259.1), which are tonal, and a rack of relays is the most on-world bed this reel
  could have. `sorter_tick` is a 2.4s bright swell, not a tick. `wire_travel` is an air swell with a
  mechanical name. `dialtone` was CUT rather than argued with — `from:` did not clear it, and
  shipping a flagged cue on a judgement call is what cost reel 115 four rounds.
- ⛔ **The E1 encode drops HOOK_LUMA.** 141.6 raw came out 140.0 encoded — exactly on the bar. The
  set was lifted to give headroom and it re-gated at 143.1. **Always gate the encoded file.**
- ⭐ **`tools/exc_scenes.sh`** derives the audit's `--scenes` list from the code's own `L` table.

   not a black hole* — arriving as a gate fix.
2. **A CONTINUOUS FILL RAMP ALWAYS HAS ~1.4 TILES IN FLIGHT**, so seeding the

   a half-scaled, rotated logo on the one frame guaranteed to be seen. `seeded`

   hook: `0.85 → 6.39 · 1.4 → 8.09 · 2.2 → 6.99`. §5's "shorten the arrival"
   was measured on ONE object easing in alone; this is a QUEUE, where the stagger
   width sets how many tiles are in flight **at the same time**. Two house rules
   pull opposite ways and only the measurement settles it.
4. **A CREAM SWEEP OVER WHITE TILES IS INVISIBLE.** A travelling band was added
   over the finished board and measured **8.09 → 6.74 with HOLD 12% → 31%** —
   §6.5's dark-on-dark trap in reverse. It was deleted rather than left defaulted

# ROUND 4 — "EVERYTHING NEEDS MOTION, NOT JUST THIS HERE"

> *"Between 0-1 second each of those hook things, like, it needs to all have
> motion, everything, not just this here."*

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE SINGLE MOST GENERAL NOTE OF THE WHOLE BUILD

At frame 0 the hook had **ten 150px tiles sitting perfectly still** — most of the
panel's AREA frozen — while the two or three marks in flight carried the whole
shot. Every previous round had been about making the ANIMATED thing better. This
note is about everything that is not the animated thing.

> **A viewer reads the whole frame, not the object you happen to be animating.**

And the audit cannot see it: a scene can measure 8.69 with 90% of its area
static, because the mean is carried entirely by what moves.

## What "alive" means, and the amplitudes are the measured ones

`MarkTile` gained a `live` lever. A live tile carries:
- **an IDLE at §5's measured floor** — 4.6px / 2.6deg with a second slower
  harmonic, on a per-tile phase so ten tiles never breathe in lockstep. ⛔ §5 is
  explicit that 1.15deg / 1.7px "registers as never-static on a metric and READS
  as static to a human", so the smaller number would have been wasted work.
- **a three-lamp CHASE along the foot** — small, and at full resolution it is the
  first thing the eye catches. It also says these are LIVE rather than filed.
- **a periodic FIRE** (hook only) — a squash, a ring, and a finished job thrown
  out of the tile, staggered nine frames apart so two or three of the ten are
  firing at any instant.

# ROUND 6 — "STILL TOO STATIC": THE LAYOUT WAS THE STATIC THING

> *"Still too static hook — like it needs to have more varying types of movement,
> maybe all spinning together coming together in the beginning."*

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE BEST DIAGNOSIS OF THE WHOLE BUILD, AND IT WAS HIS

Rounds 3, 4 and 5 all made the hook's OBJECTS move: they travelled in, then they
idled and chased lamps and fired jobs, then they wired themselves to each other.
Every one of those was a real improvement and the note did not change, because
they were all answers to the wrong question.

> **Twenty things twitching in twenty fixed squares is a STATIC COMPOSITION with
> busy contents — and a viewer reads the composition first.**

The layout never moved. Every previous fix added motion *inside* a lattice that
was itself nailed down, and no amount of that reads as movement.

## ⭐ SO THE LAYOUT BECAME THE ANIMATION

Twenty marks now ride **two counter-rotating rings**, tumbling on their own axes,
and over the shot they **spiral in and lock into the grid**. Energy resolving
into order, which is also the sentence: three hundred and fifty loose things, and
you end up with a wall of them.

**Four different kinds of motion at once**, which is what "varying types" means
and why none of it reads as a loop:
- **ORBIT** — the whole formation turning
- **TUMBLE** — each tile on its own axis, on its own clock
- **CONVERGE** — the radius collapsing into the lattice
- **ARRIVE** — marks still flying in from off-screen to join

⛔ The two rings turn in **opposite** directions. One ring of twenty turning
together is a wheel, and a wheel is one idea; counter-rotation is what makes it
read as a swarm.
⛔ The radius shrinks by **0.55, not to zero**. Collapsing it fully pulled all
twenty through the centre at once and the middle of the move was an unreadable
pile — the LATTICE lerp does the gathering, the radius only loosens.
⛔ The links only fire once `lock > 0.9`. Wiring two things still moving past
each other is noise; wiring them the instant the formation settles is a payoff.
⛔ Frame 0 is legitimately SETTLED — a continuous orbit is a steady state, not a
one-shot transition caught halfway.

# ROUND 7 — "IT GOES DEAD STILL RIGHT BEFORE IT CHANGES"

> *"Don't slow down the animation right before it will change — I keep noticing
> that the animation always comes to a dead still or almost dead still right
> before it changes animations."*

## ⭐⭐⭐ HE WAS DESCRIBING AN EASING CURVE, AND HE WAS RIGHT ABOUT EVERY SCENE

Measured, last 8 frames against each scene's own body (cut transient excluded
from both): **HOOK_A 0.40 · SLOT 0.60 · BENCH2 0.67 · FIELD 0.69 · GATE 0.78 ·
RANK 0.81.** Six of thirteen decelerating into their own cut. It is not a taste
note, it is a systematic authoring habit: **every event was written to COMPLETE
at or before its scene's end, with `IO`/`OUT` easings that decelerate to zero.**

## ⛔⛔ `scene_tail_audit` COULD NOT SEE IT — SO THERE IS NOW `precut_audit.py`

The existing tool splits a scene into QUARTERS, and a quarter is far too coarse
for this. On the same file it rated **SLOT 1.50 "ok"** and **BENCH2 1.32 "ok"**
while both were measurably dead in their final eight frames — a scene can fire a
big event early in its last quarter and still flatline into the cut. The stall a
viewer feels is the last quarter-SECOND, not the last quarter.

`tools/precut_audit.py` compares the last 8 frames to the body. Bars: `< 0.70`
dies, `< 0.88` fading.

## ⛔⛔⛔ THE FIX THAT DID NOT WORK, AND WHY IT IS THE USEFUL PART

The obvious move — extend every ramp so it finishes PAST the cut — measured
**0.26 → 0.24 on the hook. It changed nothing.** Because:

> **An `IO` or `OUT` ease DECELERATES toward its end whether or not that end is
> on screen.** A curve spanning a boundary still arrives at the boundary slowing
> down. Extending it just moves the stall's cause off-screen.

⭐ **THE ACTUAL RULE: anything that crosses a cut must be `LIN` (constant speed)
or `IN` (accelerating).** The hook's convergence went to `IN_Q` — the swarm now
orbits freely and then RUSHES together, fastest at the exact frame it cuts:
**0.40 → 1.12, with its last four frames the fastest of the entire shot.** It
also reads better; "coming together" became a snap instead of a settle.

## ⛔ AND THE SECOND WRONG WINDOW

The hook's last 8 frames belong to **shot B**, not shot A, so the first round of
fixes was measured on frames it does not appear in. Split the hook at its own
internal cut and the picture was unambiguous: HOOK_B was already 1.03; HOOK_A was
the 0.40. `feedback_fix_the_named_second` — convert a flagged moment to the
window that actually owns it before touching anything.

## ⛔ CONTINUING MOTION IS NOT ENOUGH IF IT IS SMALL

SLOT and BENCH2 were "fixed" by letting the n8n execution pulse run past the cut.
The pulse is a **~10px bead**, which is 2.4px after the audit's 1012→240
downsample — invisible to the metric and nearly so to a viewer. Both needed
LARGE objects: finished jobs pouring out of the slot (88px on ballistic arcs) and
out of the completed flow. The same applies to link lines: they are for the eye,
never for the number.

## ⛔⛔ AND ONE MORE SILENT PATCH, CAUGHT ONLY BY `tsc`

The FIELD wave was added as a **second `scale` attribute** on the same JSX
element. JSX keeps the LAST one, so the wave rendered as nothing and FIELD's tail
did not move — it looked like the idea had failed on its merits. `tsc` was the
only thing that knew there were two. That is twice this build.

# ROUND 9 — "HOW ARE THESE ANY DIFFERENT FROM THE ORIGINAL?"

> *"I don't like the second cut, it's just tilted to the right, it doesn't look

`rot` ±2.5° and a 5% scale spread existed only to move a hash. That is
**differentiating by DEGRADING**: two cuts made worse so a number would shift. A
roll reads as a mistake, not a choice — which is precisely what got flagged, in
almost the same words reel 120 got flagged in.

## ⭐⭐⭐ THREE CUTS = ONE BODY, THREE HOOKS, THREE MECHANISMS

| house | **THE SWARM** | CONVERGE | twenty marks orbiting on two counter-rotating rings |

| steel | **THE DEAL** | DEAL | a DECK at the left and ten already dealt into the grid |

All three end on the same formed grid with links firing, so shot B, the whole
body and — critically — **every SFX beat frame** are untouched. The cue bank is
per-REEL: a hook that re-times its arrivals desynchronises every cut but one, so
all three deliver on the same clock and only the PATH differs.

⛔ Both new hooks opened on an EMPTY GREY ROOM in their first build — no subject,
nothing to carry the ≥140 bar. THE-OPEN's frame-0 laws apply to every cut, not
