## Repeated hook rejections and what they teach

1. Brain-only and generic blocks did not convey the comparison. Adding marks and proof numbers still left multiple competing focal points.
2. A more hierarchical portrait/mirror treatment still lacked immediate subject action; the user requested movement and SFX from zero, plus early ChatGPT recognition.
3. A checkmark-chip around 1s made no sense. The 2s action landed and held. The free-answer graphic around 8s was confusing and blended into its background.
4. The v8 replacement passed all 18 scene-motion checks (median 9.73), yet Alex again rejected 0–1s, 2s and 9s as static/predictable and asked for a major 33s improvement. More rotation or a busier board was not the requested fix.
5. v9 replaced the operations: cover → identity; batch → press → results; shutter → name stamp; clock → cut → coin → catch → roll. The first review exposed an inert initial interval, empty transition, too-small feed action, founder-face obstruction and malformed hand coordinates. The next pass enlarged the physical press stroke, kept input papers full-size until occluded, moved the stamp below the face and fixed catch/release contact.

This preserves the rejection history. It does not claim v9 is a proven hook or that its mechanisms should be copied into every topic.
