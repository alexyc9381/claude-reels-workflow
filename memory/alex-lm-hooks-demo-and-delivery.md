# LM editing feedback and why the fixes matter

Source: Alex's LM146 revision requests, September 10–11, 2026. The requests below are explicit;
the explanations are design reasoning, **not measured retention or Instagram distribution results**.
The detailed case record is the [LM factory log on reel-lm-demo](https://github.com/alexyc9381/claude-reels-workflow/blob/reel-lm-demo/memory/reels/lm146-factory-log.md).

## Read this at the decision point

| Work | Apply | Verify |
|---|---|---|
| Hook concept and storyboard | A visible subject already performing; a second consequence after the first payoff | Frame 0, first second and seconds 3–5 at phone size |
| Tutorial body | Real setup actions, visible cursor, useful labels only | Match each spoken instruction to the target, click and resulting UI |
| Claude performance | A job, effort, contact, reaction and follow-through in the original rig | Face and contact through the entire motion, not one pose |
| Trial cuts | Different opening actions and requested soundtrack windows | Matched timestamps, actual music offsets, complete gates per cut |
| Delivery | Exact endpoint, source reproduction, named files and readback | Final encoded file, source rebuild and live destination |

## A moving camera does not establish a moving subject

Alex requested a slight opening zoom, then corrected that animation only began after the zoom.
Keep the camera push, but start the **subject's causal action at frame 0**, independently of it.
Readable identity and title should already be present; this does not mean every prop must be still.
Seed working mechanisms partway through a readable action, with travel visible immediately.
Review f0/f3/f6/f12 with and without the camera transform. If only framing changes, fix the actor's clock.
Do not hide the moving object behind a display or foreground mass.

## The first completed action needs a consequence

Alex identified seconds 3–5 after Claude finished turning the mechanism as a likely scroll-away point.
Do not answer by speeding only the first second or adding perpetual background particles.
Board the whole chain: effort → contact → visible result → Claude reacts → next scene is anticipated.
Overlap follow-through and the next action so the picture keeps changing for a reason.
Give large, readable objects real travel; a tiny light or cursor does not replace the main action.

The LM trial B R3 hook mean passed at 8.08 but hid an 18-frame near-still interval around 3.8–4.3s.
R4 enlarged the reply and added a motivated Claude jump and landing over f113–133: hook mean 8.32,
longest near-still run 3 frames. This diagnoses that render, not a universal speed target.
Read the per-frame trace and watch the exact interval. Passing averages cannot certify interest.

## Match density to the brief

LM specifically required an animation-led opening of about five seconds, then a demo-led body.
Carry a surface, frame or object continuously into the recording so the handoff feels intentional.
This **five-second recording handoff is LM-specific**, not a requirement for every future reel.
An animation-led DEPARTMENT or another topic needs its own scene logic.

For a setup tutorial, viewers need to see where to go and what to click. Record the actual app,
keep the cursor visible through movement and click, and show the resulting state. Crop around the
active controls; time zooms to the action. Side animation can react without obscuring instructions.
Do not use fake UI or unreadable full-desktop footage as evidence. Shortened waits are editorial
compression, not a speed benchmark. Preserve evidence when the working model differs from the one searched.
LM searched Qwen3.8-27B but the recorded chat used a labeled smaller Qwen3-1.7B community model.

## Every text element must earn its reading time

Alex rejected “Local AI practical demo”, “Qwen × LM Studio” and improvised top-left copy.
Use the actual house progress rail and header components. The final LM hook text was
**HOW TO RUN TOP AI MODELS LOCALLY**, all caps. That exact copy belongs to LM; the reusable rule is
to keep the user's final wording and make stage labels describe the visible action.
Remove redundant branding and slogans before shrinking useful text to accommodate them.

The right-edge correction concerns the **whole motion envelope**, including the maximum zoom,
overshoot and transition. Inspect bounds through the shot and in a phone preview, including platform
UI space. A safe initial position is insufficient when the camera later pushes the subject out.

## Claude must perform through the real rig

Preserve the original clay mascot and its recognizable proportions. Build mechanisms around actual
arm/contact locations; do not assume the sprite has articulated hands it does not possess.
Use effort and deformation before contact, a motivated expression change on the result, and a
landing or recovery afterwards. Stern → shock → cheer is useful only when those emotions fit events.
Do not turn “more expression” into a random face loop or “more motion” into constant bobbing.
Layered sets, depth and material details support the hero; they must not compete with the causal action.

## Real marks and genuinely different trial openings

Alex requested the real Qwen logo. Use a verified official asset, preserve its aspect ratio and save
its source URL. LM used [Qwen's demo asset](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Qwen/Qwen3-Demo/resolve/main/assets/qwen.png).
Avoid a hand-drawn approximation or extra brand text to compensate for a weak visual.

The trials differed in mechanism: A hoist/laptop, B horizontal feed/desktop, C assembly of keyboard,
laptop and network nodes. They used disjoint windows of the same requested track at normal music tempo:
A 13.95–58.75s, B 74.22–119.02s, C 137.41–182.21s. Those offsets belong to this edit, not a future soundtrack.
Compare actions semantically before measuring pixels. dHash is a diagnostic, not proof that Instagram
will treat a video as unique. The LM bodies intentionally matched, so whole-video minima included zero;
report that honestly even when opening-only comparisons pass.

## End and sound are part of the edit

Alex said to cut immediately on “setup”. LM ends at 44.800s / 1344 frames, with no empty end-card hold.
Locate the spoken endpoint in the audio and preserve its consonant; do not cut from ASR times alone.
Check audio, video and container duration after encoding. Future reels use their own requested endpoint.
Keep Alex's original VO at the standing 1.03× rate; do not slow it to satisfy caption metrics.
Time Foley to physical events and verify it against the actual voice/music stems. Nominal gain and
full-mix energy do not prove an effect is audible. Keep the established banned-sample checks intact.

## Finish the checks before calling a file final

LM trial B was uploaded before its complete motion report, then needed an overwrite after the dead
interval was found. Complete technical, motion, look, sound and visual review on each actual final encode
before uploading. Do not imply all gates passed when a deliberately readable demo retains motion warnings.
Check free disk space before probes/full renders; remove only verified regenerable task caches.

Deliver labeled files to the numbered reel subfolder and verify names, byte counts and IDs by readback.
Test the standalone source package by rebuilding representative frames, not just listing its ZIP entries.
For a companion article, edit the site's source DOCX and manifest, preserve the established slug, set the
spoken keyword, regenerate content and verify the real domain, article body and downloadable source.
A deployment URL alone does not prove chen.media points to the new version.
