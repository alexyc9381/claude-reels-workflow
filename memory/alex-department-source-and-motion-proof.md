# DEPARTMENT source and motion proof

Read with [Alex's LM corrections](alex-lm-hooks-demo-and-delivery.md),
[animation craft](../docs/ANIMATION-QUALITY.md), and the
[143 factory log](reels/143-department-factory-log.md).

These are production findings and interpretations of Alex's requested changes.
They are not new user quotations or measured audience-retention results.

## Resolve the source before revising a delivered movie

The DEPARTMENT movie in Drive had a later opening than the checked-in source.
The source render also revealed a large skill sheet absent from a sampled older
Drive cut. A file named FULL is not proof of source parity. Compare the actual
movie and source at frame zero, through the hook and at representative body shots.
If the later source cannot be found, record that and build a reproducible revision.
Do not claim to have reproduced the older edit exactly.

## Follow an action with an outcome

The first new hook kept the gears and lever moving, yet too little of the main
subject changed on screen. A camera push and rotating wheel did not make the
process clear. Larger skill sheets feeding into sockets, visible effort from
Claude, staggered agent landings, and distinct work products made the causal chain
readable: instructions enter, specialized agents emerge, useful work follows.

The relevant lesson is not to put sheets or machines in every hook. Choose the
physical action from the narration. The hook needs an intelligible consequence
after its first trick; otherwise the final seconds feel finished too early.
Use different shapes for different results, rather than relabeling one card.

## Check safe bounds through the whole movement

Several secondary actors and the closing keyword sat too close to the panel edge.
Fix the actor with its shadow and attached props, then check its full lean, reach
and camera push. A center coordinate inside the panel is not a safe bounding box.
Foreground scenery may crop at the edge; meaningful text, faces and logos should
remain readable. Review both native frames and a phone-sized playback.

## Expand cue helpers before measuring sound

The source SFX audit found the sample names but its perceived-level mode matched
zero explicit cues, because this reel uses frame helpers and LEVELS expressions.
Expand the actual cue array. Measure every expanded event and report the count.
Do not label an empty report as a pass. Repeated game impacts and key clicks needed
lower gains despite nominally belonging to the usual house buckets.

Full-mix energy at a cue time can be voice or music. It does not establish that
the effect is present or audible. Verify assets and timing, use an isolated SFX
render when needed, and inspect the final mix. Apply the density ceiling to a
declared event definition rather than switching between layers and unique times.

## Verify current resources at the authoritative level

The finance and legal READMEs listed fewer skills than their actual live folders.
The folders verified the narration's eight and nine. The marketing library had
grown beyond the recorded 45. Check the live inventories, preserve the spoken
recording, and make version differences clear in the guide. Do not infer that a
free skill library means free model usage.

## Test the delivered encode

The Remotion intermediate used full-range BT.601 matrix metadata. Delivery needed
a real conversion to limited-range BT.709, followed by another visual and motion
check. A tag-only change would not perform that conversion. Keep the same frame
count and tight voice ending through the audio remux.

A per-scene mean can hide a pause, and a zero exit status can hide a reported
median below the documented bar. Read the reported values, inspect an every-frame
trace, and review the action itself. Neither a number nor a contact sheet alone
establishes creative quality.
