# Trial direction and review

Status: experimental, unapproved. Alex requested this be saved to GitHub for a few videos without making it official. Keep the experiment on its own branch until he decides otherwise.

## What the feedback actually said

The starting request was layered, varied, stimulating SFX inspired by Cindy Zhu's reels, including familiar social-video sound families, physical actions and risers. Exact sample origins were not established. Do not label these her sounds or claim the individual files are currently trending.

The opening went through two opposite failure modes: dense bubbly/pitch-bending sounds blended into noise and did not make sense; the subsequent sparse correction lacked enough immediate interest. The current candidate, **Punchy action**, uses a coordinated chime + crisp attack + low body at 0:00, followed by short mechanical, paper and movement sounds aligned to the model animation. A distinct riser begins after the hook and resolves into the paper tear. The user requested more activity at frame zero and through the first two seconds; that request does not make every previous sound choice a permanent preference.

The historical FILE report documents technical checks and measured levels. It does not say Alex approved the mix, that a listening review occurred, or that retention improved. The portable opening carries that recipe; the full-length example is a clean scaffold with fewer later layers, not a bit-identical reproduction of the historical encoded video.

## Choose by action

| Visible action | Candidate family | Support only if useful |
|---|---|---|
| First-frame subject/event | One short tonal or textured accent | Dry attack + compact low body as one hit |
| Loaded mechanism releases | Ratchet ticks, latch | Small contact on the actual release |
| File moves or flips | Paper slide/turn/tear | Quiet lower texture on the same contact |
| Sprite turns/reaches/lands | Cloth, surface friction, real landing | Match material and weight; no continuous mechanical bed |
| Cursor/input/command | Click, short typing, submit tap | One distinct completion accent when the UI actually completes |
| Major reveal after the hook | Cinematic or digital riser | End on a real tear, release, opening or result |
| Race/build in progress | Short servo or operation sounds | Vary the operation, leave room for speech |
| Final approval/delivery | Stamp/contact/paper movement | Reward only when the result is visible |

Use the richer opening as a sequence with hierarchy: foreground hit, anticipation, movement, contact, consequence, brief recovery. Layers of one event share a gesture ID and serve different jobs. Keep short tails so one bright sound does not smear into the next. Thin out the body between meaningful actions; do not turn the opening density into an all-film quota. Chimes, glitches, money and game sounds should mean something in the scene.

Avoid the rejected cluster of bubbly pops, warbles, rubber bends and overlapping sparkles in the opening. The larger catalog retains audition options for other contexts; membership in the catalog is not a recommendation to use every asset. Respect older named bans outside this specifically opted-in trial; its use of short sweeps/risers is a scoped experiment, not a rewrite of the house guide.

## A small, reversible test

1. Select three videos as they are made and fill the empty slots in `trial.json`. No auto-enrollment or scheduled future runs.
2. Keep each video's original mix. Create one experimental copy with the same picture, speech and music for local comparison. Re-time to its actual movement; never reuse FILE timings blindly.
3. Review 0–2s, 0–4s and the whole reel at the same playback level. Check immediate interest, voice clarity, material/action match, variety, buildup/payoff and fatigue. Record Alex's words plus keep/change/remove decisions. More energy alone is not a pass.
4. If Alex chooses a version for a post, use the normal delivery workflow. Do not post duplicate audio-only edits merely to create a test; publication is a separate action.
5. Log available analytics at a consistent observation window, with missing metrics as null. Three different posts cannot isolate an SFX effect from topic, visuals, audience or distribution.
6. Review the trial together. Retain, revise or abandon it; only Alex's explicit later approval can make it official. No automatic promotion after three videos.

During production, inspect solo voice, music and SFX at reported problem timestamps. Compare the full context as well. Never use a metric to argue away a sound the user dislikes.
