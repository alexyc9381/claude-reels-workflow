# STORYBOARD — REEL 70 ASSEMBLY (Stage 6)
> One sentence goes in one end of a Claude-agent factory, and a finished tested feature rolls out the other.
> Format:   single dark panel · clone the CALLBACK/SERENA cinematic chassis
> Arc:      TRANSFORMATION (one overloaded generalist → a disciplined line)
> Villain:  **THE GENERALIST** — one Claude wearing four hats at once. RULE: it always guesses first and never
>           checks, and it stays undefeated (keeps shipping broken work) until a station gate REJECTS a part at S5.
> Hero cast: hero Claude (glasses) as the operator; 7 station-worker Claudes in role costumes (constr / prof /
>           chef / suit / cop / brainHat / sherlock); one sleeping Claude at S6.
> ⛔ NUMBER SPINE: 1 sentence in · 4 hats · 1 wrong guess · 7 stations · 1 feature out · 7-agent setup · ASSEMBLY
> ⛔ HERO ARTIFACT: **the work-order card.** A single note card ("add login") that physically rides the conveyor the
>    whole reel, gets stamped at each station, and rolls off the end as a finished SHIPPED crate. Everything else is
>    decoration. If the card is not on screen travelling, the scene is not paying the promise.

**Pop-culture spine:** the factory is Willy Wonka's contraption crossed with a Detroit auto line. The villain scene is
Chaplin's *Modern Times* (the man eaten by his own machine). The failure escalation is a Jenga tower on a crooked
brick. S5's gate is the red REJECT stamp of a bottling-plant QA arm.

## SCENE 1 — 0.0 to 6.5s · WIDE ESTABLISH · BEAT: HOOK
  VO:       "This is a software factory built entirely out of Claude agents. One sentence goes in one end, and a
             finished, tested feature comes out the other."
  SET:      THE FACTORY FLOOR, seen side-on. Riveted steel walls, a high clerestory window with night sky, caged
             work-lamps on chains, pipes overhead, oil-stained concrete, a hopper at frame left and a shipping ramp
             at frame right. 6 planes: sky/window → far wall + pipes → the conveyor line → hero machine bodies →
             station workers → foreground rail (out of focus, bottom edge).
  CAMERA:   STILL, locked wide. The whole line visible in one shot. No move.
  BLOCKING: Frame 0 is already mid-run. A small note card "ADD LOGIN" drops into the hopper at left. It rides right
             along the belt through the machine. At right, a crate stamped SHIPPED rolls down the ramp. Station
             Claudes work along the line. ONE focal mover: the card.
  LIGHT:    Warm amber key from the machine's interior glow, cool blue moonlight from the clerestory. Card reads
             BRIGHT against the dark belt.
  SFX:      L[0]+0.4 factory rumble bed · +0.8 hopper clunk · +2.2/+3.4/+4.6 station stamps · +5.9 ramp roll + ding
  TAKEAWAY: A machine made of Claudes turns one sentence into a shipped feature.

## SCENE 2 — 6.5 to 13.0s · MEDIUM, TIGHTER · BEAT: SETUP (villain introduced)
  VO:       "Normally you just tell Claude to build the whole thing. But that one Claude is playing researcher,
             architect, builder and tester all at once."
  SET:      ONE CRAMPED WORKBENCH, the *Modern Times* corner. The grand line is gone; a single desk buried in
             blueprints, a test rig, a keyboard, a coffee going cold, gears turning too close behind him.
  CAMERA:   STILL, closer. Hard cut from the wide.
  BLOCKING: THE GENERALIST: one Claude with FOUR hats stacked on his head (magnifier, hard hat, tool belt, lab
             coat), arms blurring between four jobs, sweating. He swaps hats faster and faster. Villain established.
  LIGHT:    Single harsh overhead bulb, swinging slightly. Everything else falls off to black.
  SFX:      L[1]+0.2 cut whoosh · +0.6/+1.4/+2.1/+2.8 four hat-swap thocks accelerating · +4.2 stress riser
  TAKEAWAY: One Claude is doing four jobs, and it is visibly too many.

## SCENE 3 — 13.0 to 19.5s · MEDIUM · BEAT: ESCALATE
  VO:       "So it guesses wrong early, and it builds your entire app on top of that guess. You think a smarter model
             would fix that, but it doesn't."
  SET:      Same corner, pulled back to a floor of cheap tile. A tower rises out of frame.
  BLOCKING: He lays ONE brick crooked (a red "ASSUMPTION" block). Then floors stack on it fast, a Jenga tower
             climbing past the top of frame, leaning further with each floor. He swaps in a shinier tool
             ("SMARTER MODEL") and the tower keeps leaning anyway. It does NOT fall yet: the villain stays undefeated.
  CAMERA:   STILL. The tower grows THROUGH the frame; the camera does not chase it.
  LIGHT:    The crooked red brick is the only saturated thing; everything above it desaturates as it climbs.
  SFX:      L[2]+0.3 brick set · +1.0..+3.4 stacking thuds accelerating · +4.6 tool swap · +5.4 groan of strain
  TAKEAWAY: One early wrong guess is load-bearing, and a better model does not un-lay the brick.

## SCENE 4 — 19.5 to 24.0s · WIDE, THE REVEAL · BEAT: TURN
  VO:       "So the people who actually ship six-figure apps build it completely differently. They split Claude into
             a line of agents that each do one job."
  SET:      Back to THE FACTORY FLOOR, now fully lit for the first time (S1 was dim and mysterious).
  CAMERA:   STILL wide. The reverse of S2's claustrophobia.
  BLOCKING: The single Generalist SPLITS: the four hats lift off him and fly out along the line, each landing on its
             own station worker. Four becomes seven as the line extends past frame. The hero move of the reel.
  LIGHT:    House lights snap on station by station, left to right, a travelling light cue.
  SFX:      L[3]+0.2 split whoosh · +0.5..+1.6 four hat-lands · +2.4 breaker throw + lights-on hum
  TAKEAWAY: The fix is separation: one job per agent.

## SCENE 5 — 24.0 to 33.0s · TRACKING THE CARD · BEAT: ESCALATE (the mechanism + the villain's defeat)
  VO:       "The researcher locks in the facts and hands it off to the builder. The builder writes the code and hands
             it off to the next person, but nothing moves down the line until the station signs it off."
  SET:      The line at working height: stations with hanging numbered signs, in-trays and out-trays, a stamp arm.
  CAMERA:   ONE motivated move — the reel's only lateral track, following the card left to right. (This is the 1 of
             the 2-3 permitted moving scenes.)
  BLOCKING: The card passes hand to hand: RESEARCHER stamps FACTS LOCKED into an out-tray → BUILDER stamps CODE →
             at the third station the gate arm slams a red REJECT and the belt STOPS. Beat. The part goes back one
             station, gets fixed, returns, gets a green SIGNED OFF, and only then does the belt move again.
             ⭐ This is where the villain finally loses: the gate catches the thing the Generalist never would.
  LIGHT:    Each station lights as the card arrives and dims behind it, so the card is always the brightest thing.
  SFX:      L[4]+0.5 stamp · +1.8 stamp · +3.2 REJECT slam + belt halt (the loudest hit of the reel) · +5.0 rework
             clicks · +6.2 green chime · +6.6 belt restart
  TAKEAWAY: Nothing advances until a station signs it off, which is why the output is not a guess.

## SCENE 6 — 33.0 to 40.0s · WIDE, NIGHT · BEAT: PAYOFF
  VO:       "So you give the system one feature, and you just watch it travel down the line. And what rolls off the

  SET:      The far end of the line at 4am. A window going from night to first light. A chair to the side.
  CAMERA:   STILL wide.
  BLOCKING: Hero Claude asleep in the chair, one small lamp on. The line runs itself behind him. The crate rolls off
             the ramp, lands, and pops open to reveal the working feature (a real UI panel, lit). A green PASSED tick.
             Dawn creeps across the floor.
  LIGHT:    Cold night → warm dawn wash travelling across the floor as the crate lands. The travelling light IS the
             sustained mover here.
  SFX:      L[5]+0.4 belt roll · +2.6 crate land thud · +3.0 latch pop · +3.6 UI power-on chime · +5.2 dawn swell
  TAKEAWAY: It finished without you.

## The three floors (spec §2) — how this board clears them
1. **Real place, not shapes on black.** Every scene is one of two committed locations (the factory floor / the cramped
   workbench), each with 6 named depth planes, a committed light direction, and world props.
2. **Camera disciplined.** Six of seven scenes are LOCKED. Exactly ONE motivated move in the whole reel: the S5
   lateral track following the card, which is motivated because the card is the subject.
3. **Arc has shape, payoff not spent early.** Intensity: S1 7 (spectacle) → S2 5 (claustrophobic) → S3 6.5 (the tower)
   → S4 8 (the split) → S5 **9, the REJECT slam is the peak** → S6 8 (dawn payoff) → S7 8.5 (CTA). No belly sag; the
   peak beats the hook; the villain is undefeated until S5.

## ⛔ Gate check
- The 7 agents are NAMED as a line but the wiring, prompts and handoffs are never shown. S7 shows them sealed.
- Nothing on screen is copy-pasteable. The card says "ADD LOGIN" and nothing more.
