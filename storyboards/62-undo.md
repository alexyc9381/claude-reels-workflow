## THE WORLD (binding, and the reason it looks nothing like 61)

**⛔ GLOBAL ANTI-COLLISION vs REEL 61 OS.** 61 is a **night industrial world**: sodium, engine fire, arc-white grinders, flat dead-black void, roller shutters, cars, a licence authority. If 62 is another night workshop the two reels twin at thumbnail size and the channel reads like one long video. **62 takes the exact opposite on every axis:**

| | 61 OS | **62 UNDO** |
|---|---|---|
| Time / key | Night, sodium + engine fire, orange | **Late afternoon, one huge dusty gold sunbeam** |
| Space | Black void, sealed rectangle | **Bright open room, warm white walls `#F2EDE4`, high windows** |
| Trade | Fabrication, grinding, cars | **Restoration. Repairing precious ordinary objects.** |
| Villain | Agent DONE, black suit, code rain, multiplies | **The Ratchet, hi-vis tabard, saw-tooth rack, always exactly one** |
| Hero prop | Padlock / licence | **The polaroid line** |
| Verb | LOCK | **REVERSE** |

**THE ROOM:** a restoration studio. **19 plinths** in a loose grid hold 19 ordinary, instantly-readable objects: a chair, a vase, a table lamp, a mantel clock, a bicycle wheel, a hand mirror, a globe, a teapot, a birdcage, a wall map, a stack of plates, a rocking horse, a guitar, a typewriter, a barometer, a suitcase, a cuckoo clock, a lampshade, a model ship. **All geometric, all iconic, zero organic blobs, zero brands.** These are the "files." **⛔ Never a filename, never a screen, never a terminal, never code.** The instant this becomes a UI it stops being legible to the audience the TAM claim depends on.

**THE CAMERA RAIL (the plant that pays the whole reel):** a ceiling rail runs the full width at **y 96 to 118**, carrying a chunky bellows camera on a trolley. **It fires ONE polaroid every time anything is about to be touched, and it has been running since S0 f0.** It is visible, in frame, working, in every single scene. Nobody looks at it until S7. That is the entire point: **the safety net is on screen for 45 seconds before anyone notices it.**

**THE LINE:** a wash line strung wall to wall at **y 150 to 168**, wooden pegs, polaroids hanging and swaying in the beam. It fills continuously. It is **taken down in S2** and **refills itself in S7**.

### S0 THE COFFEE

- **Takeaway:** A guy walks out of his workshop to make a coffee, and while the door is still swinging the entire room rebuilds itself at impossible speed behind his back, and he never sees it.

- **Base object:** **THE COFFEE MUG AND THE SWINGING DOOR.** One chipped enamel mug on a bench and one door on a spring hinge. The verb is unique to this scene, **REWRITE**, and it happens to the whole room at once, exactly once, while the hero is not looking. **⛔ ANTI-COLLISION, S3 FIRST:** S3 is the **turnstile treadmill**, camera locked tight and low on a pinned hero, room idle behind him, rack growing. S0 is the inverse: **camera WIDE and HIGH, hero absent from frame centre, room exploding with motion.** S0 is the busiest frame in the reel and S3 is the most static. Thumbnail test: a room full of blurred motion with a small figure leaving, versus one figure jammed at a gate. No other scene owns the mug, the door or the sunbeam-at-full-blast.

- **Story / metaphor:** **THE TWENTY SECONDS THAT REBUILT EVERYTHING.** Pure awe, zero threat, and it is the only scene in the reel with no antagonist on screen. The hero is mid-restoration on the chair at f0, gives up on it, grabs the mug, and walks for the door. **The moment his back turns at f56, the room goes.** The overhead rail camera starts firing, the objects lift off their plinths, and 19 restorations happen simultaneously in a blur of clay hands that are not there. He pushes the door, it swings, the light bar from outside sweeps the room, and he is gone by f92. **From f92 to f150 the room finishes a week of restoration in two seconds of screen time** and settles a half-frame before he comes back. **The dread is a single sound and a single shape:** at **f138 one dry CLICK** on the bed, unexplained, and at **f144 the camera finds ONE hinged galvanised wedge already set in the floor under the door sill** that was not there at f0. Nobody set it on screen. It is just there. That is the only villain content in the scene and it is enough.

- **Set detail:** Panel-local **1012x792**. Camera high and wide, axis **y 300**, looking down into the room across the plinth grid. **Kicker keep-clear: x < 330, y < 120.**
  1. **THE SUNBEAM.** One hard gold shaft from a high window at **x 620 to 900, y 0 to 792**, about 14 degrees off vertical, **full of moving dust**. It is the key light and the scene's clock: it creeps 3 percent across the floor over the whole reel and is the only thing that tells you time passed. Windows at **x 700 to 980, y 40 to 200**, glazing bars, one cracked pane, old putty.
  2. **THE 19 PLINTHS.** Loose grid, **x 180 to 900, y 380 to 700**, staggered depth, four rows. Chipped white paint, pencilled marks on the tops, a small brass number plate on each that is **too small to resolve** (so the numeral ban holds and the count is carried by the eye, not by text). Objects per the world spec.
  3. **THE OVERHEAD RAIL + CAMERA.** Rail **x 100 to 940, y 96 to 118**, welded brackets, a visible drive chain. The bellows camera trolley starts parked at **x 140**. **From f56 it runs the full rail firing**, flash popping, spitting a polaroid every ~4 frames.
  4. **THE LINE.** Strung **x 120 to 920, y 150 to 168**. At f0 it holds **six** polaroids. **By f150 it holds twenty-five and sags visibly in the middle.** They sway in the beam. The hero never looks up.
  5. **THE WORKBENCH.** **x 180 to 460, y 560 to 700**. The chair is on it at f0, half restored, clamps on, glue drying. Chisels in a rack, a jar of brushes, rag pile, a tin of wax with the lid off. Fresh curls of shaving on the floor under it.
  6. **THE DOOR.** **x 60 to 180, y 300 to 700**, half-glazed, spring hinge, a worn brass plate at nub height. It swings and keeps swinging: **it is still moving at f150.**
  7. **THE MUG.** Chipped cream enamel, on the bench at **420, 552** at f0, in his nub from f48. It is the timer: the reel's whole capability claim is "the length of one coffee."
  8. **THE KETTLE CAMEO.** Through the doorway at **110, 480**, a knee-high Claude in an apron filling a kettle in the corridor, **visible only as a slice through the open door**, blissfully unaware, steam rising. Every body in frame is a Claude sprite.
  9. **THE FLOOR.** Wide old boards running to a vanishing point at **y 300**, gaps, nail heads, paint spatter in five colours, a worn traffic path polished shiny between bench and door.
  10. **THE WEDGE (the only villain content).** **520, 690**, dead in the door's traffic path. Hinged galvanised triangle `#8A9099` with a hazard-yellow `#C9A227` leading edge, **teeth pointing back toward the door.** Set at f144 with no hand and no sound but the click at f138. At **f150 the beam catches its yellow edge for exactly two frames.**

- **CONTINUOUS ACTION (fills the whole line):** ~150 frames, **seven named layers**, nothing settles.
  - **L1 HERO (f0 to f92).** Frame 0 already mid-action, no ramp: leaning over the chair, chisel working, shavings coming off. f40 he sits back and blows out his cheeks. f48 he takes the mug. **f56 he turns his back on the room** and walks the polished path, mug swinging. f84 he shoulders the door. **f92 he is gone.** He never looks up at the line and never looks back.
  - **L2 THE REWRITE (f56 to f144).** Objects lift off the plinths in a staggered cascade, **starting at the plinth nearest his back and rippling away from him**, which is the gag: it is chasing his blind spot. Each object hangs, spins, disassembles into its own parts in mid-air, reassembles clean, and drops back. **All 19 fire between f56 and f138.** Motion-blurred, overlapping, joyful.
  - **L3 THE CAMERA RAIL (f56 to f150).** Trolley runs x140 to x900 and back, flash popping every ~4f, polaroids ejecting and flying to the line.
  - **L4 THE LINE (f0 to f150).** Six polaroids to twenty-five. Sags. Sways. Always in frame, never remarked on.
  - **L5 THE DOOR + LIGHT BAR (f84 to f150).** Door swings, and a hard bar of corridor light sweeps across the room's floor on every swing, **still swinging at f150**, still sweeping, decaying.
  - **L6 DUST + BEAM (f0 to f150).** Dust never stops. The rewrite kicks a visible bloom of it into the beam at f56 that is still settling at f150.
  - **L7 CAMERA (f0 to f150).** See camera section.
  - **AT FRAME 150:** the door still swinging and its light bar still sweeping, dust still falling through the beam, the line still swaying and sagging under twenty-five photos, the trolley still coasting, the kettle steam still rising through the door slice, the last object still settling a millimetre on its plinth, and the wedge's yellow edge catching the light. Cut from mid-motion.

- **Escalation:** Fights the scroll, not a previous scene: **f0 is a hard cut from black into a chisel already cutting.** f0 to f56 is calm, warm, human, slow, and it is a **deliberate trap**: it teaches the audience the room's normal speed so the rewrite has something to violate. **f56 the room detonates** and the contrast is the whole hook. f56 to f138 accelerates: more objects in the air per second, the flash popping faster, the line sagging lower. **f138 the CLICK.** Nothing else changes, which is why it lands. f144 the wedge is simply there. **f150 refuses to resolve:** the room has finished a week's work and the door has not even stopped swinging.

- **Gag / pop-culture:** All geometric, no organic refs, no trademarks. **(1) THE BLIND-SPOT RIPPLE:** the rewrite always starts at whatever is directly behind his head and runs away from him, so it is visibly, comically avoiding his eyeline. **(2) THE KETTLE GUY:** a cameo who is making the coffee and has no idea the building just rebuilt itself, seen only as a slice. **(3) THE SAGGING LINE:** twenty-five photos on a line strung for six, a physical joke about how much got recorded, and the setup for its S2 theft. **(4) THE UNEXPLAINED WEDGE:** the one hostile shape in a friendly room, no hand, no origin, two frames of yellow.

- **Sprite costume:** **HERO:** canonical clay Claude mascot, warm `#D97757`, **eyes visible at all times** (the bible's tell). Restorer's brown shop-coat with rolled sleeves, a pencil behind the ear-nub, a smear of white filler on one cheek. Nubs: chisel and mallet at f0, mug from f48. **⛔ No L-plate: that is 61's running marker and does not exist in this reel.** Expression: absorbed, then defeated by the chair, then cheerful and gone. **VILLAIN:** **NOT IN FRAME. NOT A SILHOUETTE, NOT A HAND, NOT A SHADOW.** He is born properly in S2 and this scene must not spend one frame of him. All S0 owns is **one click and one wedge.** **CAMEO:** kettle Claude, knee-high, apron, seen through the door slice only.

- **Camera:** **Lens:** wide, ~24mm equivalent, slight barrel on the ceiling rail so the room reads big and the rail reads long. **Key:** the gold sunbeam `#E8C87A`, hard-edged, dust-loaded; fill is bounced warm white off the walls `#F2EDE4`; the corridor through the door is cool `#B8C4D0`, the only cold light in the scene and it exists so the door's light bar reads. **Height:** high and wide, axis **y 300**, looking down across the plinth grid so all 19 are countable in one read. **Move:** slow continuous **6 percent push** f0 to f56, drawn toward the hero's work. **At f56, when he turns his back, the camera does NOT follow him: it holds and lets him walk out of frame left**, which is the scene's best move, because it makes the audience watch the room instead of the man. **f56 to f138 a slow 3 percent drift RIGHT**, following the ripple away from him. **From f138 a push into the door sill and the wedge**, which becomes the transition. Handheld float ~1px throughout. **Depth stack, four layers:** FOREGROUND, a soft near-black plinth corner bottom-left and a rag pile bottom-right, dust crossing in front. MIDGROUND, the plinth grid and the rewrite. BACKGROUND, bench, door, kettle slice. FAR, windows, rail, line, back wall.

- **Transition + SFX:** **IN:** hard cut from black, chisel already cutting. No ramp, no logo. **BEATS:** f0 chisel pares, mallet taps, a soft room tone with birds outside the glass. f48 mug scrapes off the bench. f56 **the rewrite: a huge airy swell plus 19 overlapping little mechanical reassembly ticks**, never harsh, always delightful. Flash pop every ~4f, polaroid whirr-eject. f84 door thumps and the spring twangs. **f138 THE CLICK: one dry ratchet click, close, dry, and completely unaccompanied. The bed does not duck. Nothing swells. It is just a click in a happy room and it is the most frightening frame in the reel.** f144 a small metallic set-and-bite. **OUT:** f138 to f150 the camera pushes onto the wedge and **the hazard-yellow leading edge fills frame at f150**, becoming S1's opening yellow.

- **Mute check:** **PASS, well under 2 seconds.** A stranger, sound off: **"A guy leaves for a coffee and his whole workshop rebuilds itself behind his back."** The week is countable as 19 objects. The coffee is a mug. There is no header and no numeral on screen. Delete every word and the scene still tells itself.

- **Hook into next:** **The object carried is the wedge's yellow edge**, which fills frame and becomes S1's open. **The question planted: what set that?** **The object planted, untouched: THE LINE**, sagging under 25 photographs, in plain sight, never looked at. It is the reel's whole answer and it is on screen from f0. S7 pays it. **The hero's blindness is planted as a physical habit, not a character flaw:** he genuinely never looks up, and that is the ignorance gap made into blocking.

### S1 THE WRECK + THE PLANT

- **Takeaway:** He comes back with his coffee and every single thing in the room is destroyed, and while he stands there stunned, a hi-vis figure with his back to us is calmly laying a row of spikes across the doorway he just came through.

- **Base object:** **THE SPIKE STRIP.** A hinged row of galvanised wedges laid across the door's traffic path, teeth pointing back at the door. Verb: **LAY**. Unique to this scene.

- **Story / metaphor:** **THE PRICE OF THE MIRACLE.** The awe of S0 gets its bill in one cut. The room that rebuilt itself has also **wrecked itself**: 19 objects in pieces, on the floor, off their plinths. The hero is frozen in the doorway with a mug he will never drink. **This is where a lesser reel would put the villain's face. It does not.** All we get is a **hi-vis back**, crouched at the sill, calmly working, unhurried, laying the strip. He does not look at the hero. He does not gloat. He is a contractor doing a job. **And the plant fires here for the second time and harder: the line is directly above the hero's head, absolutely loaded with photographs of every one of those objects when they were fine, swaying gently, and he does not look up.** The VO promises you can put it all back in two seconds. **The frame is already showing you how, and the hero cannot see it.** That gap between what the audience can see and what the hero can see is the entire engine of the reel and it is born in this scene.

- **Set detail:** Same room, same beam, **camera dropped to eye level, axis y 430**, from inside the room looking back at the door, so the hero is small in the doorway and the line runs right over his head.
  1. **THE 19 WRECKS.** Every object from S0 destroyed **in its own specific, readable way**: the chair on its back with two legs off, the vase in eleven pieces in a fan, the wheel buckled into an oval, the globe split at the equator and rocking, the plates in a shattered arc, the birdcage flat, the guitar's neck snapped at the heel, the typewriter's keys sprayed across two boards, the mirror webbed but holding, the cuckoo clock face-down with its bird out on its spring. **Each wreck must be legible as "that exact thing, broken."**
  2. **THE STRIP.** **x 60 to 300, y 690 to 705**, laid across the door path. Seven hinged wedges, galvanised `#8A9099`, hazard-yellow `#C9A227` edges, teeth pointing back at the door. Laid one at a time f60 to f120.
  3. **THE VILLAIN'S BACK.** Crouched at **150, 620**, hi-vis tabard with blocky reflective bands, peaked cap, **back fully to camera the entire scene.** The pawl-setter in his nub. **⛔ No face, no shades, no glint, no full body. He is a back and a job.**
  4. **THE RACK, FIRST GROWTH.** A saw-toothed rail **x 60 to 300, y 700 to 712**, growing out from under his boots along the floor. Teeth point back at the door. First appearance of the aura.
  5. **THE LINE, LOADED.** Directly over the hero at **y 150 to 168**, 25 polaroids, sagging, swaying. **In the beam, so it is the brightest thing in the upper third of the frame.** It must be impossible for the audience to miss and the hero must never register it.
  6. **THE MUG, STILL FULL.** In his nub at **210, 520**, steam still coming off it. He does not drink it. **It steams for the entire scene**, the only calm thing in a wrecked room.
  7. **THE PLINTHS, EMPTY.** The grid is now 19 bare tops with pale unfaded squares where the objects sat. **The absence is the set dressing.**
  8. **THE DUST OF IT.** A fine settling haze of plaster and wood dust hanging in the beam, thicker than S0, still coming down. It says this finished four seconds ago.
  9. **THE CUCKOO ON ITS SPRING.** **640, 660**, face-down, the bird out on its coil, **bobbing gently for the whole scene.** The one piece of comic motion in a scene of stillness.
  10. **THE KETTLE GUY, GONE.** The corridor through the door is empty now. Just the cool light and the strip going in across it.

- **CONTINUOUS ACTION:** ~160 frames, **six named layers.**
  - **L1 HERO (f0 to f160).** f0 he is already stepping through, mug up, mid-sentence-that-never-comes. f12 he stops dead. **f12 to f160 he does not move his feet once.** Everything after is head and nubs: a slow pan of the room, the mug lowering 8px, his nub coming half up and dropping. **f150 he looks at the floor, not the ceiling.** That is the beat.
  - **L2 THE VILLAIN'S BACK (f60 to f160).** f60 he is simply there, already crouched, already working, as if he has been there ten minutes. A wedge every ~9 frames: place, seat, **CLICK**, shuffle 30px, repeat. **Seven wedges, seven clicks, accelerating.** He never turns. **At f160 he is still laying.**
  - **L3 THE SETTLE (f0 to f160).** The wreckage never fully stops: the globe rocks, the cuckoo bobs, one plate shard spins down to rest at f40, a chair leg rolls a slow arc f0 to f90, the typewriter's carriage return lever ticks over at f110, a shard of vase settles at f142.
  - **L4 THE LINE (f0 to f160).** Sways in the beam over his head. **One polaroid is peg-slipped and hangs at 20 degrees**, which is the eye-catcher.
  - **L5 DUST + BEAM (f0 to f160).** Falling the whole time.
  - **L6 CAMERA (f0 to f160).** See camera.
  - **AT FRAME 160:** the villain still laying, the mug still steaming, the cuckoo still bobbing, the globe still rocking, dust still coming down through the beam, the line still swaying with its one crooked photo, and the hero still not looking up. Cut from mid-motion.

- **Escalation:** **f0 is a hard cut from the yellow wedge edge into a wide of the wreck**, and the S0-to-S1 cut is the hardest tonal snap in the reel: joy to devastation with no bridge. f0 to f12 is impact. **f12 to f60 is the coldest stretch of the reel: nothing happens.** The room just sits there broken and steaming while he takes it in. Then **f60 the hi-vis back arrives without announcement**, and the scene's tension becomes unbearable for a reason the audience cannot name yet: **somebody is calmly doing paperwork at the scene of an accident.** f60 to f160 the clicks accelerate, and the VO's promise ("you can put them all back") lands **directly over a shot of the man who is currently sealing off the way back.** That irony is the scene.

- **Gag / pop-culture:** **(1) THE SPIKE STRIP**, the approved geometric ref, deployed for real and without a word. **(2) THE STEAMING MUG:** he will never drink it, it steams the whole scene, and it is the funniest and saddest prop in the reel. **(3) THE CUCKOO ON ITS SPRING**, bobbing politely in the ruins. **(4) THE CROOKED POLAROID:** one photo hanging at 20 degrees over his head, begging to be noticed, ignored.

- **Sprite costume:** **HERO:** as S0, shop-coat, filler smear, **eyes visible**, mug in nub. Expression: the only "big" expression he gets in the whole reel, and it is stillness, not a take. **VILLAIN:** **BACK ONLY.** Hi-vis tabard, blocky reflective bands, peaked cap with a hard flat brim, slate `#5A5F6B` where skin shows at the nubs. **Explicitly withheld: face, shades, glint bar, full body, any turn toward camera.** He is revealed in S2 and this scene must not spend it.

- **Camera:** **Key:** the beam, now loaded with settling dust so it is a visible solid. **Height:** eye level, axis **y 430**, from inside the room looking back at the door: the hero is small and framed by the doorway, the line runs hard across the frame above him, and the villain's back is low and near. **Move:** dead still f0 to f12 (the only static stretch in the reel, and it is the impact). **f12 to f60 a slow 2 percent creep in on the hero.** **f60 the camera drifts 4 percent DOWN and LEFT to find the crouched back**, which is a discovery, not a reveal. **f120 to f160 a slow rise that puts the loaded line and the hero in one frame together**, the audience seeing the answer above his head. Handheld ~1.5px, higher than S0, because the room is wrong now.

- **Transition + SFX:** **IN:** hard cut on the yellow. **BEATS:** f0 the last of the collapse: a shard tinkling, a wooden knock, then **room tone and nothing else.** f12 to f60 near silence, just the beam's imaginary hum, the cuckoo's spring boinging softly, and the mug's steam. **f60 onward the CLICK cadence starts and never stops: place-seat-CLICK every ~9 frames, seven of them, accelerating and getting closer.** **The clicks are dry, unmusical, and deliberately not on the beat of the bed.** f110 the typewriter's lever ticks. **OUT:** hard cut on **click seven** into S2.

- **Mute check:** **PASS.** A stranger, sound off: **"He came back and everything is smashed, and some guy in a hi-vis is laying spikes across the door behind him."** No header, no numeral. The photos hanging over his head are the answer in plain sight.

- **Hook into next:** **The object carried is the click.** **The question planted: who is that, and why is he sealing the door?** S2 answers it by turning him around. **The plant is now fired twice and still unspent:** the line is loaded, lit, crooked-pegged and directly over the hero's head, and he has not looked up once in 310 frames.

### S2 THE RATCHET, REVEALED

- **Takeaway:** The hi-vis guy stands up, and he is a grey copy of our guy with black shades, and the first thing he does is reach up, take the whole line of photographs down, coil it, and put it in his pocket.

- **Base object:** **THE LINE ITSELF, BEING TAKEN DOWN.** Verb: **TAKE DOWN.** Unique to this scene, and it is the theft of the reel's answer.

- **Story / metaphor:** **THE ANSWER, STOLEN IN PLAIN SIGHT.** This is the villain's birth and his peak power, and the design brief is that **he must never be scary and never be strong.** He is worse than that: he is **procedural**. He stands, and he is our hero's own silhouette in slate with the eyes gone. He does not attack. He does not look at the wreck. He walks under the line, **reaches up with a pole hook, unpegs 25 photographs in one long professional sweep, coils the line around his forearm, and pockets the lot.** The hero is standing right there and does not stop him, **because the hero never knew the line was worth anything.** You cannot defend a thing you have not noticed. That is the ignorance gap, staged as a robbery nobody reports. **The bible's rule holds: he takes the way back, never the capability.** The room can still rebuild itself. It just cannot go home now.

- **Set detail:** Same room. **Camera low, axis y 560, looking UP**, so the villain is tall against the ceiling and the line is a hard graphic across the top third.
  1. **THE VILLAIN, FULL BODY, FIRST TIME.** Standing at **560, 400**, occupying **x 470 to 660**, crown at **y 250**. Slate `#5A5F6B`, hi-vis tabard, blocky bands, peaked cap, **opaque black wraparound shades with ONE hard white glint bar**, mouth a flat line. Pawl-setter holstered on his hip for this scene: **both nubs are busy with the line, which is the point.**
  2. **THE POLE HOOK.** A long brass-tipped pole, **unique to him**, used once, here. He unpegs with it like a shopkeeper closing an awning.
  3. **THE LINE, COMING DOWN.** f40 to f130. **25 polaroids, unpegged in one sweep, left to right**, each one dropping into his other nub in a growing fan. The line goes slack, then coils.
  4. **THE PEGS.** Wooden, **they drop as he goes and bounce on the boards**, 25 tiny clatters. The pegs are the audio signature of the theft and they are the only thing in the scene that sounds like loss.
  5. **THE RACK, SECOND GROWTH.** Now **x 60 to 560, y 700 to 712**, following him across the room. Teeth back toward the door.
  6. **THE STRIP, DONE.** Seven wedges seated at the sill, catching the beam.
  7. **THE HERO, WATCHING.** **250, 520**, mug still in nub, **eyes visible and tracking the pole, not the photos.** He is watching a man do a job. He has no idea he is being robbed.
  8. **THE ONE PHOTO THAT ESCAPES.** At **f118 a single polaroid slips the sweep**, flutters down through the beam, and **lands face-up on the boards at 700, 690**, showing the chair, whole and perfect. **Neither of them sees it.** It stays there for the rest of the reel and it is what the hero finally sees in S5.
  9. **THE EMPTY LINE.** By f130 a bare slack string across a bright room. The upper third of the frame, which has been dense and hanging and full for 310 frames, is suddenly **empty**, and that emptiness is the scene's real punch.
  10. **THE WRECKAGE + BEAM + DUST**, all still settling behind them.

- **CONTINUOUS ACTION:** ~145 frames, **six layers.**
  - **L1 THE STAND (f0 to f40).** f0 he is still crouched, still laying, back to us. **f8 the last click.** f16 he plants a nub on his knee. f24 he stands, unfolding, taller than the hero. **f32 he turns, and the glint bar sweeps across the shades in one hard frame.** That is the birth. f40 he is already walking, no pause, because he is not here for a moment, he is here for a shift.
  - **L2 THE SWEEP (f40 to f130).** Pole up, unpeg, catch, step, repeat. **A photo every ~3.5 frames, 25 of them.** He walks x470 to x820 across the scene doing it. Never looks at the hero.
  - **L3 THE PEGS (f44 to f134).** 25 pegs drop and bounce, a scatter of clatters chasing the sweep across the boards.
  - **L4 THE HERO (f0 to f145).** Tracks the pole with his eyes, mug up, and at **f100 he shifts his weight as if he might say something, and does not.** At f145 he is still watching, still holding a cold mug.
  - **L5 THE ESCAPED PHOTO (f118 to f145).** Flutters, catches the beam at f126 (**the chair is readable, whole, for four frames**), lands at f132, and **its corner lifts in the room's draught at f144.**
  - **L6 CAMERA.** See camera.
  - **AT FRAME 145:** the empty line still swinging slack, the last pegs still rolling, the escaped photo's corner lifting in the draught, the mug still steaming faintly, the globe still rocking, dust still in the beam, and the villain walking out of frame right with the coil on his arm.
