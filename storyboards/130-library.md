## THE WORLD — **THE PROMPT COUNTER**

An all-night **issue counter**: a lit service window at the front, a shutter, and behind it a deep
**picking hall** whose back wall is ranks of numbered card drawers on a moving rail. You walk up with
a job, you name it, and a finished card comes over the counter. You do not make one.

It is the machine the subject actually is — *"copy-paste prompts, tagged by task and role"* is a
**counter transaction**, not a reading room — and it is the shape memory asks for by name:
[[feedback_a_transaction_not_a_conveyor]] — *"you ask and you GET x" needs the transaction on screen.*

### The mapping table (`THE-OPEN.md` — every row must fill in)

| on screen | what it actually is |
|---|---|
| a Claude at a bench **cutting a card by hand**, a spike of 40 identical ones beside him | re-prompting Claude from scratch, every single time |
| the **BLANK DOCKET** on that spike | the prompt you write longhand because you have nothing saved |
| the **shutter** he has his back to, and has never opened | the library page, published, free, and unvisited |
| the **PICKING WALL** behind it — ranks of drawers, already loaded | the 52 prompts already written |
| each drawer's **coloured tab strip** | the prompt's category (Understand / Debug / Review / …) |
| **five banks** across the wall, each with its own light | discover · design · build · ship · operate |
| the card's **punched slots** and their red guide line | the prompt's fill-in fields, `{path}` and `{behavior}` |
| the **STOCK HATCH** at the back, where Anthropic's own crew loads the wall | the prompts are lifted from Anthropic's own workflow / best-practice / team guides |
| a **labelled BUTTON** on the console that fires one card, forever | a slash command — `/name` from `.claude/skills/<name>/SKILL.md` |
| the **ROUTE BOARD** lighting the whole run before a single card moves | `Shift+Tab` → plan mode: it maps the project before it writes code |
| the **SETUP PLATE** bolted to the console, read at the start of every shift | `CLAUDE.md`, loaded at the start of every session |
| the counter Claude **spinning a finished card to face you** | copy-paste |

No row reads "it just looks cool". The literal layer sits on top throughout: the real command strings,
the real `Shift+Tab` mode line, the real file paths, the real category names, and the Claude mark.

### ⛔ Distinct from reel 112 SQUAD (THE SUMMONING FLOOR)
112 shipped a **lit library hall** with repos as **bound volumes** and a barrage of books. The word
"library" is in this reel's CTA and must not pull the world back there. **There is not one book, one
shelf, one spine or one reading room in reel 130.** The unit is a **CARD**, the building is a
**COUNTER AND A PICKING HALL**, and the verb is **ISSUE**, not *borrow*.

### ⛔ Distinct from reel 127 DESIGN (THE BOARD WORKS)
127 was an industrial hall with a **press** stamping plates. 130 has no press, no plate stamping and
no belt: its motion is **drawers, a picking rail, a chute and a counter**, and its light is a warm
service window against a cool deep hall, not a foundry.

### ⛔⛔ THE ONE THING THE VO SAYS THAT THE FRAME DOES NOT CLAIM

> *"there's already over a hundred free templates"*

**The official Claude Code prompt library holds 52.** So `COUNT_BANNED` greps for `100`, `100+`,
`OVER 100`, `HUNDREDS` and `1000`, and **no plate, band, chip or drawer face in this reel prints a
total above 52.** The line is paid instead by the PICTURE: at S9 the wall is revealed running past
the crop on both sides with its five bank counters still climbing — *more than you can count* — which
is true of the wall as drawn and asserts no number. The receipt `52 PROMPTS · 15 CATEGORIES` is spent
at S2, where the VO is describing what the library IS.

⛔ `PERF_BANNED` = `10X` · `X FASTER` · `SAVES` · `%` · `BEST` · `SOTA` — no speed or saving claim
has a source. ⛔ `PAID_BANNED` = `$` · `PRO ONLY` · `SUBSCRIPTION` — it is free and nothing hints
otherwise.

## THE OPEN (`docs/THE-OPEN.md` — scene 0 is authored to its own spec)

Three hard-cut shots in 3.83s. Frame 0 is bright, saturated, populated, and carries the claim.

**SHOT A · f0–f40 (1.33s) · THE BENCH.** Locked. A Claude under one sodium lamp is **hand-cutting a
card** — a real down-stroke on a lever cutter, a stamp, a scribble — with a **spike of forty identical
hand-cut cards** beside him and a fresh blank already waiting. He is `heat` 0.5: flushed, trembling,
steaming off the crown. Behind him, filling the frame, a **closed roller shutter** with the Claude
mark cast into it. *Recognition, with no narration: this is you, re-typing the same prompt.*

**SHOT B · f40–f76 (1.20s) · THE SHUTTER GOES UP.** Hard cut, wider. The shutter **rips upward in 6
frames** — a real accelerating pull, slats stacking, grit falling through the light — and behind it is
the **PICKING WALL**, already lit, already loaded, ranks of tabbed drawers running past the top and
both sides of the crop, a rail crossing it and crew working. He turns; `shock` 1; the saw stops
mid-stroke. *The arrival costs something: a floor-shaking land, a dust wash, two rings.*

**SHOT C · f76–f115 (1.30s) · THE ISSUE.** Punch to the counter. The counterhand **slams a finished
card down and spins it to face camera**: category tab, printed prompt, punched fill-in slots, red
guide line, the Anthropic mark in the corner. `FREE` stamps into the corner on the spoken word.

Four laws: **(1) BRIGHT** — the sodium lamp, the cream claim plate and the lit wall keep frame 0 over
140. **(2) THE SUBJECT IS IN IT** — a Claude and the Claude mark are both on frame 0. **(3)
RECOGNITION** — hand-making a thing that already exists in bulk five feet behind you. **(4) A REASON
TO STAY** — the wall is revealed and then *not explained*; the first card is issued at 2.5s.

## THE THREE FLOORS (`STORYBOARD-SPEC.md` §2)

1. **Every scene is a real place.** Eight named places, each with ≥4 depth planes (back wall · wall
   face · rail · counter · floor · kerb · an occluder cropped by the frame edge). Every scene has a
   mass cropped by the panel edge in front of the action.
2. **The camera is disciplined.** Locked everywhere but the standing in-panel push; **exactly one**
   motivated re-framing move in the reel (S6's pull-back as the route outgrows the board), plus the
   hook's own S0b→S0c punch. One subject moves at a time.
3. **The arc has a shape and the peak beats the hook.**
   `9 · 6 · 7 · 7.5 · 6.5 · 8 · 8.5 · 7.5 · 8 · 10 · 8.5 · 9`
   No belly sag: the only dip is S4, one 1.94s beat, deliberately the darkest and quietest in the
   reel so S5 has somewhere to come up from. The antagonist is never beaten — it is simply not needed
   after S3, and it is still in the last frame.
