# STORYBOARD — REEL 78 LIMITS (Stage 6)
> **Logline:** you keep hitting Claude Code usage limits because three specific things are burning your budget — here is each one, and the switch that stops it.
> **Format:**   single dark panel (1012×792 at left 34 / top 384) · clone the **77 TESTED** chassis verbatim
> **Arc:**      **value-first, three-rung ladder** (STORY-ARCS.md). No villain — see note below.
> **Villain:**  NONE. The limit is a *condition*, not an antagonist. A villain that loses three times in a row violates villain integrity, so the antagonist role is carried instead by a recurring **motif** (the gauge) that stays in the red until the peak.
> **Hero cast:** the tester (hero sprite, 330px) · a Claude sprite per scene, costumed to the setting (copy clerk / drafter / surveyor). Max 2 actors on screen.
> ⛔ **NUMBER SPINE:**   `LIMIT` → `1 OF 3` → `2 OF 3` → `3 OF 3` → `CLAUDE`
> ⛔ **HERO ARTIFACT:**  **the usage gauge.** Buried in the red at the hook, untouched through the middle, and swung back into the green only at S5. It is the only object allowed to appear in more than one scene, and it is what makes the peak beat the hook.

**⛔ No fabricated numbers.** The gauge is a needle position, never a percentage. The VO makes no numeric claim and neither does the screen. The only digits on screen are the `n OF 3` counter and the tricks themselves.

## S0 — HOOK · 0.00 to 4.36s · **THREE VARIANTS, PENDING APPROVAL**

  VO: *"If your Claude Code keeps hitting usage limits, here are three tricks to make it run way more efficiently."*

All three are built to the same floor: a real place, frame 0 complete, camera locked, one large mover,
header in a white box in the top third, and the gauge planted so S5 can pay it off. They differ in
**interaction paradigm**, not in set dressing — per [[reel-idea-generic-diagnosis]], varying the noun is
not varying the idea.

### VARIANT A — THE PIT LANE  ·  paradigm: **depletion**
  SET: a night pit garage. Oil-dark concrete floor with painted lane markings, a breeze-block back wall,
  a race car up on jacks at frame right, tyre stacks, a tool trolley, sodium light spilling from the
  open lane mouth behind. Wall-sized fuel gauge dominating the back wall — the hero artifact, planted.
  CAMERA: locked wide, eye level. No move.
  BLOCKING: f0 — the tester stands at the gauge, fuel line in hand, needle already deep in the red arc.
  Claude-mechanic crouched at the car. f10 — **the needle snaps hard to E** (the large mover, sweeping a
  third of the frame), the garage floods red from an unseen warning lamp, the tyre stacks throw new
  shadows. f24 — the mechanic's head comes up. Nothing else moves.
  LIGHT: single sodium key from lane-left, warm; the red warning wash is the second source and arrives
  as an event. Hero reads lighter than the block wall.
  TAKEAWAY: you are running out, fast, and you can see exactly how fast.

### VARIANT B — THE SHUTTER  ·  paradigm: **barrier**
  SET: a lit workbench at night in a small workshop. Scarred wooden bench top, pegboard back wall with
  tools in outline, a half-finished job clamped in the middle, shavings, an anglepoise throwing a hard
  pool of white. Deep shadow beyond. The gauge is a small panel meter screwed to the pegboard — planted
  quietly, so S5's reveal enlarges it.
  CAMERA: locked, slightly above bench height. No move.
  BLOCKING: f0 — the tester mid-reach for the next tool, the job visibly unfinished. f8 — **a steel
  roller shutter slams down out of frame-top through the middle of the bench** (the large mover, full
  frame height), cutting the work in half and severing the light pool. f20 — a red `LIMIT` plate stamps
  onto the shutter face. His hand is still out, on the wrong side.
  LIGHT: one hard anglepoise key from frame-left; after the shutter, the right half of the frame loses
  its key entirely and goes cold. The light change *is* the story beat.
  TAKEAWAY: it does not slow down, it stops you, and it stops you mid-job.

### VARIANT C — THE COPY ROOM  ·  paradigm: **waste made visible**
  SET: an office copy room at 2am. Grey vinyl floor, one big copier under a cold fluorescent tube,
  a paper tower already stacked to the ceiling beside it, recycling bins, a window black with night.
  The gauge is the copier's own toner/usage strip on its front panel, buried in red.
  CAMERA: locked, wide enough to hold the full tower. No move.
  BLOCKING: f0 — the tester holds up **one single sheet**, the tower looming behind him. f12 — he feeds
  the one sheet in and **the machine reprints the entire tower** (the large mover: a column of paper
  erupting up and over), sheets avalanching across the floor. f28 — he is knee-deep, still holding the
  one page.
  LIGHT: cold fluorescent from directly above, one direction, hard down-shadows. Hero reads warm against
  a blue-grey room.
  TAKEAWAY: you are paying to reprint everything, every single time, to ask for one thing.

## S2 — TRICK 1 why · 6.86 to 18.97s (12.11s) · **THREE LOCKED SHOTS** · ESCALATE → TURN

  VO: *"Claude rereads your entire history on every message, so a compact squashes it all into a tight summary and you keep going without losing the important parts."*

  ⚠️ This is the longest beat in the reel by 4s. **It is not one shot.** Per [[reel-multishot-structure]],
  it cuts into three locked framings, each advancing the idea. No camera move in any of them.

  **Shot A (6.86–11.0) — THE REREAD.** Same wide. The tester types one short line at a terminal beside
  the copier; the machine answers by pulling the *entire* tower through itself again, page by page, a
  visible upward sweep. He waits. The point: one message, whole history re-read.
  **Shot B (11.0–15.2) — THE COMPRESS.** Hard cut to a tighter locked framing on the tower alone. A press
  descends and the tower **collapses to a single slim folder** (the reel's largest single mover: a
  ceiling-height column reduced to hand-height in one motion). Dust puffs. `/compact` never appears as
  text — the effect is the explanation.
  **Shot C (15.2–18.97) — THE KEEP-GOING.** Hard cut back to the wide. The slim folder sits where the
  tower was. He types again; this time the answer comes back immediately. Three pages float clear of the
  folder and settle on top, still legible — *without losing the important parts*.
  LIGHT: unchanged overhead fluorescent across all three, so the cuts read as the same room.
  SFX: paper riffle (shot A), a single heavy press-thump (shot B, the beat's peak), one soft chime (shot C).
  TAKEAWAY: compaction is not deletion — it is the same room with the weight taken out.
