# STORYBOARD — REEL 124 WEB (Stage 6)

> **Logline:** every AI site builder hands back the same flat page; one tool hands back a
> place you can walk into, from one prompt.
> **Format:** single dark panel · clone the house chassis (SlopKit `Bg`/`Panel`/`HookHeader`/
> `KaraokeCaption`/`ProgressBar` + WorldKit `Surface`/`Occluder`/`Cone`), reel 111 LIBRARIES for
> the real-capture technique (`SiteScreen`, `PlainPage`).
> **Arc:** transformation (flat → deep). Villain present but never gloated over.
> **Villain:** THE TEMPLATE PRESS — the machine that stamps the same page forever.
> Its RULE: it never breaks and it is never smashed. At S10 it is simply **out-built**: it is
> still stamping in the background of the payoff, ignored. (`feedback_villain_integrity`)
> **Hero cast:** one clay Claude throughout (the builder). Crews of 4–6 at S5 (the agency floor)
> and S10 (the launch). Costumes cycled deterministically via `costumeFor(i)` across all 12 levers.
> ⛔ **NUMBER SPINE, in order:** `LANDING-PAGE-01` ×3 (the press's identical output) · `1 PROMPT` ·
> `5 LAYERS` (the delamination count) · `3 MONTHS` vs `1 PROMPT` (S5) · `dora.run` (the address bar,
> every real-UI shot) · `0 LINES OF CODE` (S9) · `SECONDS` (S10) · `WEB` (CTA).
> ⛔ **HERO ARTIFACT:** **THE DEEP BAY** — one arcade frontage that starts as a single flat sheet
> and ends as a five-layer lit diorama you can see *into*. Everything else is decoration.

## THE WORLD — **THE ARCADE**

A covered night arcade. Wet dark stone floor with practical reflections, a receding colonnade of
piers, a glazed barrel roof with visible ribs overhead, and along both sides **BAYS** — shop
frontages whose contents are websites.

* A **flat** bay is a painted-shut poster in a frame: one plane, no interior, dead.
* A **deep** bay is a lit diorama box: 4–5 planes at real separation, 3D objects standing between
  them, the whole thing parallaxing as anything moves past it.

Why this world and not another (`reel-theme-must-map-to-mechanic`):

| on screen | what it actually is |
|---|---|
| a bay frontage | a website |
| a painted-shut poster in a frame | a flat AI-generated page |
| a lit diorama with 5 separated planes | a 3D scroll site's depth layers |
| walking down the arcade | scrolling the page |
| the diorama's objects turning to follow you | "elements that actually respond as you scroll" |
| the press stamping identical sheets | "every builder gives the same template" |
| the prompt card posted into the bay | "from a single prompt" |
| the arcade lighting up end to end | launch |

Every row fills in. **Depth is not decoration here — depth IS the product**, which is why the set
engine (`WorldKit.Surface` + `Occluder`) is doing story work rather than atmosphere.

**Light:** one committed direction per scene, always a practical *in* the set (bay light, press
work-lamp, the roof ribs). Ground stays dark (p10 ≤ 35); the SITES are the bright, saturated things.
Body scenes target luma 70–105 / sat ≥34%; the ≥140 bar is **frame 0 only** and is carried by the
press fascia, never by the hero.

## The three floors

1. **Every scene is a real place.** One named world (THE ARCADE), 5 depth planes everywhere, one
   committed light direction per scene, an `Occluder` cropped by the frame edge in every scene —
   the check §8 says is the only honest one for depth.
2. **The camera is disciplined.** Every scene is LOCKED. The only travel in the reel is the HERO
   (S3) and the LIGHT (S10). Zero camera moves; the per-scene in-panel push is the house rig only.
3. **The arc has a shape and the payoff is not spent early.** Intensity:
   `S0 9 · S1 5 · S2 8.5 · S3 8 · S4 5.5 · S5 7 · S6 7.5 · S7 8.5 · S8 7.5 · S9 8 · S10 10 · S11 6`
   No belly sag (the floor is 5 and it is used only on the two 1-second setup/contrast beats), and
   the peak (S10 = 10) beats the hook (S0 = 9).

## Adversarial critic pass

| the attack | verdict |
|---|---|
| **Swipe points 0–5s** | 0.0 the press already loaded and a stack already there · 0.9 first slam · 1.9 second, the stack passes his waist · 2.9 third, he is boxed in · 3.4 the card goes into the slot. No second repeats the one before it. |
| **Repeated base-object** | The BAY appears in S2/S3/S7/S8/S10 — flagged. Fixed by giving each its own hero object and framing: S2 front-on delamination · S3 side-on with a travelling hero · S7 three *different* bays · S8 a floor GAUGE · S10 the arcade's full length. `feedback_one_prop_five_scenes` — checked as a strip, not one at a time. |
| **Payoff spent early** | S2 shows the mechanism, not the product. The first finished, real, lit site is S6, and the whole street is held back to S10. |
| **Villain integrity** | The press loses exactly once, at S10, and only by being ignored. It is never smashed. |
| **A cut is not an event** | Every scene names one event with before/trigger/travel/arrival. S7 is the only multi-cut scene and each of its three cuts carries a different clause of the sentence. |
| **§3 container test** | Run per scene on the VERB: *gives* → a machine issuing · *generates* → a sheet delaminating · *respond* → objects turning to follow · *gives you flat* → a knock that does not move · *took 3 months* → a calendar tearing · *handles* → three arms doing three jobs · *launch* → lights coming up. No scene draws a box with a label on it. |
| **§12 "what does the CLAUDE do"** | S0 works the press and gets buried · S1 posts the card · S3 walks the whole bay · S4 knocks · S5 the crew works · S6 scrolls · S8 rides the gauge · S10 walks in with the crew. No scene has him standing while things happen around him. |
| **Text budget** | ONE text chip per shot, in the reserved plate band (panel y 112–210). The number spine lives on objects that had to be drawn anyway — a fascia, a die face, a gauge, an address bar. |

## ⛔ Honesty guard

* `dora.run`'s own claim is "Start with AI, ship 3D animated websites without code" — that is what
  the reel depicts and it does not go past it.
* **Lovable / Replit** appear only in the two lines that name them, as marks on tiles beside OUR
  drawn flat page. Their real captures are never shown under a pejorative claim, and no reference
  site is ever shown under "this is what you built".
* Reference captures (awwwards' 3D and WebGL walls, and the animation-library sites) are used as a
  WALL of many in the hook-adjacent and payoff frames, so no single studio is implicated.
