**MOTION 14.23 · HOLD 23%.**

⛔ **THE RINGS OPEN TOWARDS CAMERA — a horseshoe, not a circle.** A closed ring

## ⭐⭐⭐ ROUND 9 — ONE MAIN THING, RANKED (0-8s)

*"Make each of the animations way more hierarchical and focused on ONE main thing. Between 0-8
seconds way more interesting, right now it's not good."*

This is the exact counter-pressure to the previous four rounds. Every note before it was answered
by ADDING — crowd bands, formations, fly bars, star piles, five hero intros — and the frames ended
up busy and **unranked**. `feedback_hook_simplicity` is explicit: five objects competing across a
frame is a ROOM; a hook is an IMAGE, and hierarchy is what a viewer reads in the first 200ms.

Each of the first three scenes is now stripped to one subject with everything else demoted:

| scene | the ONE thing | what was removed |
|---|---|---|
| **S0 IRON** | the curtain lifting | the 15-bulb cornice run, the drifting motes, and three of the five marching ranks — the company is now TWO ranks painted close in value so it reads as one MASS, not eighty individuals |
| **S1 CRATE** | the crate erupting | the fly bar of lanterns, the near-camera crowd band, and the floor-wide star pile (now a compact mound on the crate itself). The crate is centred and scaled 1.62 -> 2.05 |
| **S2 LINEUP** | the five hero landings | the background formation, which turned five ranked landings into a wall of clay |

⭐ **AND THE HONEST WAY TO PAY FOR IT.** Stripping S1 cost it 12.01 -> 7.38: fewer competing
objects, but also far less swept area. The wrong fix is to put the competitors back. Scaling the
subject barely helped (7.38 -> 7.48) because **a big STATIC object contributes nothing to a
frame-difference metric** — the crate sits still while only its lid, the burst and the mound move.
A continuous in-panel **PUSH** repaints every pixel every frame without adding a single object:
**7.48 -> 8.40.** That is the lever whenever the note is "one main thing" and the one thing is
large and static.

## ⭐⭐ ROUND 8 — THE SUPERHERO INTRODUCTION (0-10s)

*"Between 0-10 seconds a lot of these animations need to be so much revised and improved, to show
like an agency, maybe kind of like a SUPERHERO INTRODUCTION."*

A specific and very good register for this subject, and the one that finally answers something
every earlier round circled: **a sprite standing in a crowd has no identity.** The repo's whole
pitch is 273 NAMED characters with personalities, and the character-select / team-assemble intro
is the film language that says "a roster of specialists" instantly.

`HeroIntro` is a four-part beat, hard-cut, about 14 frames each:
1. a hard SPOTLIGHT snaps down out of the dark (a shaped cone, never a full-frame wash)
2. the character DROPS in and lands in a power pose, with a squash and dust
3. a NAME PLATE slams in from the side carrying the REAL title and division
4. a colour ring bursts and the light settles

⛔ The pose is the Mascot's own `cheer` rig, not hand-drawn limbs — reel 110 spent two rounds
hanging geometry off the body and it read as a tail.

**S2 became THE LINE-UP:** five hero landings in a fast run, then the whole line poses together and
the count locks at 273. *"You get a massive team of specialist agents"* is a ROSTER line, and five
staggered landings say "a team" where one crate said "a delivery". **15.33, HOLD 61% -> 17%.**

**S3's three named agents get the same treatment**, minus the plate — the dressing-room lintels
already carry their names, and two plates for one character is the duplicate-receipt defect one
layer up. **11.08 -> 12.17, HOLD 25% -> 17%.**

⛔ **Two layout bugs, both found in a still:** the plates were drawn BELOW the characters and fell
off the panel floor (the ground line is y706 and the panel ends at 792, so there is never room
under a standing sprite), and at 264px wide on a 190px pitch the five overlapped by 74px and read
as ONE strip. Narrowed to 178 and staggered high/low, which is also more like a comic.

## ⛔⛔ ROUND 7 — A PROCESS IS NOT AN EVENT

*"At 5 seconds that scene sucks... there's not actually a beginning and end part for each scene,
it's literally just bouncing up and down doing nothing."*

The sharpest note of the build, and it names a trap I walked straight into one round earlier. When
two scenes came back weak I gave them **CONTINUOUS AMBIENT PROCESSES** — stars raining forever, a
belt running forever — because continuous motion scores well on the audit. But a process has no
before state, no trigger and no arrival. **Nothing ever happens in it. It is weather.**

⛔ And the motion audit is BLIND to this: ambient rain measured 14.72 and an event that resolves
measures 12.01. The column that sees it is **HOLD**, and it was at 71% and 61%.

Both scenes are now §2's four parts:

| | before | trigger | travel | arrival |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 BURST** | a shut crate carrying the GitHub mark, counter at 0 | the hero heaves the lid and it flies off | the stars ERUPT from inside it and arc down | the last one lands, the pile settles, the count LOCKS at 149,734, the licence stamps |
| **S2 CRATE** | an empty floor, counter at 0 | a crate falls and SLAMS | it bursts apart in two halves | the team is standing where it landed, count locks at 273 |

**HOLD 71% -> 31% and 61% -> 22%.** Motion went DOWN on S1 and that is the correct trade: weather
that never stops always outscores a beat that finishes.

⭐ And the sound followed for the fourth time: a heave, a lid off, the fall, the settle, and a
three-deep layer on the crate slam.

## ⛔⛔ ROUND 6 — "EVERYONE BOUNCING AROUND" IS A NAMED FAILURE

*"The hook is not interesting whatsoever... it's just everyone like bouncing around, not
interesting. It actually has to attract the attention."*

That is **AN ACTION LOOP IS NOT A SCENE** (docs/ANIMATION-QUALITY §10), word for word. The curtain
lift was a real event, but it finished at f50 and what it revealed was 85 sprites each running PACE
or HOP **in place**. Everybody was busy and nothing was happening — the same note reel 110 got on
eight sprites, earned here on eighty-five.

⛔ **AND IT WAS A ROOM, NOT AN IMAGE.** 85 sprites + 18 division boards + a marquee + a curtain is
four ideas competing in a shot that is allowed one. The 18 boards are gone from the hook; that
countable content belongs in the body, where a viewer has time to read it.

⭐⭐⭐ **THE MASS NOW DOES ONE THING, IN UNISON, TOWARD CAMERA: IT MARCHES OUT.** Five ranks, four
synchronised STEPS, each a discrete land with a squash, a stomp and dust — §1's "N discrete pops
beat one long tween" at the scale of a whole company. Each step brings them closer and bigger, the
hero is downstage in the near plane cropped at the knees, and the frame is fullest on the last beat.

**Two arithmetic corrections inside it, both the same law:**
- ⛔ Growing each sprite 26% per step against a FIXED span merged the company into one clay mass by
  the last step — `reel-sprite-grounding-law` broken by ANIMATION rather than by layout. The rank
  now SPREADS as it advances so `spacing >= 0.85 x size` holds at every step.
- ⛔ At 78px per step they marched OUT of frame and the shot EMPTIED on the beat it should have
  peaked on. 46px advances them visibly and lands the final step inside the panel.

⭐ And the sound followed the action for the third time: the bank was still firing lamp snaps at
f20/34/48 against a shot whose action is now four stomps. They land on f26/41/56/71 — the frames
the ranks actually land on — and ascend.
