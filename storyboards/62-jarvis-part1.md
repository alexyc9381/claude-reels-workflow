## THE ARC
Setup (S0–S2): an empty helmet and a promise → Build (S3–S5): three memories pour in → Proof (S6): it cost
nothing → **PEAK (S7–S8): it speaks in your voice and quotes you back to yourself** → Resolution (S9) →
Series hook (S10) → CTA (S11).
**Intensity:** 8 → 6 → 6.5 → 7 → 7.5 → 8 → 5 → 9 → **10** → 8.5 → 7.5 → 8.5.
Peak at **S8 (the note)**. The S6 dip to 5 is deliberate — one breath of quiet before the rehook so the peak
has somewhere to climb from. No mid-sag: S3→S5 climbs continuously.

## ⭐ THE VISUAL ENGINE — THE HELMET FILLS UP
A knockoff **IRON CLAUDE** helmet sits on a workbench in a dark workshop, **eyes dead black**. Every memory
source poured in makes the eyes glow one step brighter and more of the HUD draw itself inside the visor. By
S8 the helmet is fully alive. **The helmet IS the progress bar** — the viewer can read "how far in are we"
off the eyes alone, on mute, at any frame. That is why this reel does not need a literal progress bar
competing for the frame; the reward seal at the CTA ([[reel-progress-bar-reward]]) is the helmet's final
power-on.
**WHY this engine:** the script's whole claim is *accumulation* (notes + meetings + sent mail = a person).
An object that visibly FILLS is the only metaphor that makes accumulation mute-legible in <2s per scene.
**REJECTED:** a chat window filling with text (unreadable on mute, and it is the shot every AI reel already
uses); a brain graphic (cerebral, and "brain" as a literal picture reads as a stock icon, not an artifact).

## ⭐ THE RUNNING GAG — THE GAUNTLETS IN THE CORNER
A pair of **unbuilt IRON CLAUDE gauntlets** sits in the corner of the bench from **S0 onward**, in shot,
untouched, slightly out of focus, every single scene. The mascot keeps *almost* reaching for them and going
back to the helmet. In **S10** the camera finally pushes to them as the VO says *"part two, I give it hands."*
**This is the series' cross-episode open loop, made physical** — and it is the one thing Luke's 13 posts never
had: a visible reason episode 2 exists. Part 2's board opens on the gauntlets.

## POP-CULTURE CAST ([[reel-knockoff-references]])
**IRON CLAUDE** — the mascot in a knockoff Iron-Man chest-plate, but *never* the full suit (the suit is the
series' end-state, not episode one's). **DUM-B** — a knockoff one-armed workshop robot who is the comic
bystander: fumbles a wrench in S1, tries to help in S5 and gets waved off, faints in S8. **Living world:**
workshop lights flickering on bank by bank as the helmet powers up, dust in the key-light, a slow-turning
ceiling fan, sparks from an off-screen welder.

## NUMBER SPINE (never drifts)
**3** (links) · **10** (seconds each) · **8** (months, the note). ⛔ Do NOT put "10 parts" on screen — say
"part one" only. Committing to ten publicly is the Luke trap; the checkpoint is part 3.

## ⭐ HOOK — 3 VISUAL CONCEPTS ([[reel-storyboard-process]]: visual options, not VO alternates)
- **A ⭐ RECOMMENDED — THE BOOT.** Black. A dead helmet. The eyes **SNAP** on and the HUD floods the visor in
  one hard beat, lighting the whole workshop. Frame-0 claim `NO CODE` already burned. *Why it wins:* CLAIM-
  BEFORE-SPECTACLE — the claim is readable at 0.0s and the 10-energy spike fires AFTER ~1.0s of claim time
  (the BLUEPRINT pattern; VAULT died doing the reverse).
- **B — THE ASSEMBLE.** Plates fly in and build the helmet around the mascot's head in ~1s. *Risk:* it is a

- **C — THE WALL.** Three glowing links snap into a socket board, helmet lights. *Risk:* reads as a settings
  screen. Weakest.
Build A; wire B/C behind a `hook` prop as trial variants.
