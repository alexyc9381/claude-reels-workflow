## THE WORLD (binding)

**⛔ GLOBAL ANTI-COLLISION vs REEL 61 OS.** 61 is a **night industrial world**: sodium, engine fire, arc-white grinders, black void, roller shutters, cars, a licence authority. **62 takes the opposite on every axis:**

| | 61 OS | **62 TAKES** |
|---|---|---|
| Time / key | Night, sodium + engine fire | **Interior, no daylight, hard theatrical stage lamps `#F0E2C0` in a black-velvet void** |
| Space | Sealed rectangle, black void | **A vast sound stage with depth, five bays in a row, receding** |
| Trade | Fabrication, grinding, cars | **Filmmaking. Shooting takes.** |
| Villain | Agent DONE, suit, code rain, multiplies | **Take One, backward cap, clapperboard, blackout trail, always exactly one** |

**THE SOUND STAGE:** a working film stage. **THE JOB is a physical thing being made on a set**, not code, not a screen: **a chair.** One chair, built on camera, every take. It is the perfect job object because a stranger can grade a chair in half a second (this one wobbles, this one is beautiful) with zero explanation. **⛔ Never a terminal, never a prompt, never a UI.** **THE ATTEMPT is a reel of film.** **THE GRADE is a verdict.**

**THE FIVE BAYS:** five identical stages in a row, **x 60 to 950**, receding slightly, separated by solid flats. Bay 1 is lit from f0. Bays 2 to 5 are **dark and dressed identically** and the audience can just about make them out from S0, which is the plant: **the stages were always there.**

### S0 PRINT IT

- **Takeaway:** A guy finishes building a chair on a film set, and before he can even look at it a grey copy of him slams a clapperboard, stamps it PRINT IT, and starts switching all the lights off.

- **Base object:** **THE CLAPPERBOARD READING TAKE 1.** Hinged slate, diagonal stripes on the clapstick, chalk dust. Verb: **SLAM**. Unique to this scene, and it is the only time in the reel he gets to use it successfully.

- **Story / metaphor:** **THE DAY THAT ENDS AT TAKE ONE.** The hero is a director-builder mid-shot, genuinely enjoying himself, finishing a chair under hot lamps. It is a **fine** chair. Not bad, not special. **And the instant his hands leave it, the board SLAMS.** Take One is simply there, cap backward, viewfinder swinging unused on his chest, and he stamps **PRINT IT** on the reel can in smug gold-ochre and starts walking. **Wherever he walks, the lamps go out behind him**, bay by bay, because once he has called print there is no reason to shoot anything else. The hero does not fight him. **The hero agrees with him.** That is the horror: he looks at the fine chair, shrugs, and accepts it, because he has never once seen the alternative. **And behind them both, briefly catching the last of the light before it dies, are four more stages, dressed identically, never used.** The audience sees them. The hero never turns around.

- **Set detail:** Panel-local **1012x792**. Camera mid, axis **y 460**, looking down the row of bays so the four dark ones recede behind bay 1. **Kicker keep-clear: x < 330, y < 120.**
  1. **BAY ONE, LIT.** **x 60 to 420, y 200 to 700.** Hot theatrical key `#F0E2C0` from a lamp rig overhead, a painted flat behind, chalk tape marks on the deck, a sandbag on every stand leg, cable snaking to a distro box.
  2. **THE CHAIR.** **240, 560**, on a mark. Finished at f30. **It is FINE.** Four legs, sits level, plain. **⛔ Do not make it bad. The scene dies if the chair is bad.** The whole point is that take one is acceptable, which is exactly why nobody ever shoots take two.
  3. **THE CLAPPERBOARD.** In Take One's nub at **520, 500**. Reads **TAKE 1**. **Slams at f44** with a hard wooden crack.
  4. **THE REEL CAN + THE STAMP.** A flat circular film can at **600, 640**. **PRINT IT** lands on its lid at f58 in gold-ochre `#C9A227`, crooked, smug.
  5. **THE FOUR DARK BAYS.** **x 440 to 950**, receding, **dressed identically to bay 1**: same flat, same marks, same stand, **and an unbuilt chair kit stacked flat on each mark.** They catch the edge of bay 1's spill for the first 60 frames and then go fully black as the blackout runs. **This is the reel's central plant and it must be visible without being pointed at.**
  6. **THE LAMP RIG.** Overhead truss **x 60 to 950, y 96 to 130**, hung lamps with barn doors, safety chains, a visible dimmer run. **Lamps go out one by one f60 to f130**, tracking his walk.
  7. **THE DIRECTOR'S CHAIR SILHOUETTE.** Wherever Take One stops, a canvas chair-back shape sits behind him at nub height, unlabelled. It is a shape, not a prop, and it never moves with him. Petty authority as set dressing.
  8. **THE DECK.** Scuffed black stage floor, chalk marks in four colours, gaffer tape lifting at the edges, a stray sandbag, one cable run taped down badly with a trip loop.
  9. **THE SLATE BOARD ON THE WALL.** **740, 250**, a hanging production board with **rows for takes 1 to 5**. **Row 1 filled. Rows 2 to 5 blank.** Sub-resolution scribble, no readable digits, but the shape of four empty rows reads instantly.
  10. **THE GRIP CAMEO.** **150, 660**, knee-high Claude in a tool belt and ear defenders, already coiling cable at f0, **wrapping up before anyone told him to**, because on this set the day always ends at take one. He is done and gone by f120.

- **CONTINUOUS ACTION:** ~130 frames, **six named layers.** Nothing settles.
  - **L1 HERO (f0 to f130).** Frame 0 is already mid-action, no ramp: seating the last chair leg, mallet in nub. f30 he sets the mallet down and his hands come off. **f36 to f44 he leans back to LOOK at it**, and that is the beat the board interrupts. f44 he flinches at the clap. **f60 he looks at the chair, then shrugs**, and the shrug is the saddest frame in the reel. f70 to f130 he starts tidying, agreeing, complicit. **He never turns around.**
  - **L2 TAKE ONE (f36 to f130).** f36 he is simply there at 520,500, no entrance, already raising the board. **f44 SLAM.** f58 the stamp. **f66 to f130 he walks x520 to x950**, unhurried, and the lamps die behind him.
  - **L3 THE BLACKOUT (f60 to f130).** Lamps out one at a time, ~14 frames apart, **tracking his walk exactly**. The four dark bays go from dimly visible to gone.
  - **L4 THE GRIP (f0 to f120).** Coiling, stacking, killing a stand, gone by f120.
  - **L5 DUST + HAZE (f0 to f130).** Stage haze in every lamp cone, moving. As lamps die the haze loses its shape bay by bay, which is how the audience feels the room shrink.
  - **L6 CAMERA (f0 to f130).** See camera.
  - **AT FRAME 130:** the hero still tidying with his back to the dark, the last lamp still dying, haze still drifting in the one cone left, the PRINT IT stamp still rocking on the can lid, the trip loop of cable still lying there, and four black bays where four stages used to be visible. Cut from mid-motion.

- **Escalation:** Fights the scroll, not a previous scene: **f0 is a hard cut from black into a mallet already swinging.** f0 to f36 is warm, competent, pleasant, and it is a **trap**: it teaches the audience that this is a good day. **f44 the SLAM is the hardest transient in the first four seconds** and it lands on the exact frame the hero leans back to look at his own work, so **he never gets to see it.** f58 the stamp. **f60 the shrug, and the audience's stomach drops before they can explain why.** f60 to f130 the blackout is a slow, calm, total loss of possibility, and the last thing to disappear is the four dressed stages nobody used. **f130 refuses to resolve:** the hero is happily tidying up inside a shrinking box.

- **Sprite costume:** **HERO:** canonical clay Claude mascot, warm `#D97757`, **eyes visible at all times** (the bible's tell). Expression arc: absorbed at f0, **hopeful at f36**, flinch at f44, **shrug at f60**, agreeable at f130. **VILLAIN:** the `<Villain/>` component per the bible, **fully revealed from f36, no build-up**: slate `#5A5F6B`, backward flat cap, unused viewfinder on a lanyard, clapperboard, stamp. **He is not a mystery in this reel.** Unlike Agent DONE he does not need a birth: he arrives at peak power in the first four seconds, because **he has already won every day of your life so far.** **CAMEO:** grip Claude, knee-high, tool belt, ear defenders.

MIDGROUND, hero, chair, Take One. BACKGROUND, the four bays. FAR, the truss, the production board, the black void.

- **Transition + SFX:** **IN:** hard cut from black, mallet already landing. **BEATS:** f0 mallet on wood, a satisfying seat-and-tap groove, stage-lamp hum, a distant genny, the grip's cable slapping the deck. **f44 THE SLAM: one hard wooden CRACK, close, dry, and the bed ducks hard around it.** f58 a fat rubber stamp THUD. **f60 to f130 the blackout: eight lamp-clunks, each one a heavy contactor thunk plus a filament ping as it cools**, tracking his walk, and **the room tone gets smaller with every one.** f120 the grip's last stand collapses with a clatter and his footsteps leave. **OUT:** on the last lamp's clunk into S1's darkness.

### S1 THE TRAILER DOOR

- **Takeaway:** There is a second grey copy standing in a trailer doorway holding a chair that is obviously better, and Take One is leaning across the door so he cannot get out, and our guy never turns round.

- **Base object:** **THE TRAILER DOORWAY.** A lit rectangle in the black with a figure in it. Verb: **BLOCK**. Unique.

- **Story / metaphor:** **THE BETTER ANSWER, TWENTY FEET AWAY.** This is the scene that makes the reel hurt, and it works entirely on dramatic irony. **Take Two exists.** He is right there, lit, holding a chair that is visibly, unarguably better than the one on the mark: same job, different solve, and even a stranger can see it in half a second. **He is trying to come to set.** And Take One is leaning one arm across the doorway, not even looking at him, bored, the way a man holds a door shut with his body while reading something else. **The hero is thirty feet away with his back to the whole thing, packing up, happy.** He does not know Take Two exists. He never will. **That is the ignorance gap, staged as a man in a doorway.** The VO says you get two different answers and one is better and you never find out. **The frame shows you both, and shows you exactly who is stopping you.**

- **Set detail:** **Camera low and wide, axis y 620**, from behind the hero's ankles, so he is a large soft foreground silhouette and the lit trailer door is small and sharp in the far distance. **The whole scene is one deep composition.**
  1. **THE TRAILER.** **x 600 to 820, y 300 to 700** in the black, a boxy production trailer, steps down to the deck, a rectangle of warm light spilling from its door at **x 660 to 760, y 380 to 690**.
  2. **TAKE TWO, IN THE DOORWAY.** **710, 500**, slate `#5A5F6B`, **identical to Take One in every way except one: no cap, no board, no stamp.** He is holding **the better chair**. He is half a step forward, weight committed, going nowhere. **⛔ He must be visually identical to Take One and to the hero. Sameness is the reel's thesis. He is not a different character, he is a different attempt.**
  3. **THE BETTER CHAIR.** In Take Two's nubs. **It must be legibly better at thumbnail size and by shape alone:** the same chair with an obvious, elegant improvement a stranger reads instantly (a graceful curve where the first was a blunt right angle). **⛔ No decoration, no colour trick, no sparkle. Just better geometry.**
  4. **TAKE ONE'S ARM.** **640, 480**, one nub braced across the doorway at chest height. **He is not looking at Take Two.** He is looking out at the dark stage, bored, board hanging from his other nub.
  5. **THE HERO'S BACK.** Large foreground silhouette **x 100 to 340, y 380 to 792**, soft, near-black, **packing a toolbox, shoulders relaxed.** He occupies a third of the frame and he is the least aware thing in it.
  6. **THE FINE CHAIR**, on its mark at **300, 620**, in the last working pool of light, alone.
  7. **THE FOUR DARK BAYS**, still there, still dressed, now barely readable in the black. Present, never lit.
  8. **THE PRODUCTION BOARD**, rows 2 to 5 still blank.
  9. **THE HAZE**, now only alive in the trailer's door-spill and the one chair light. Two small pools in a big black room.
  10. **THE STEPS.** Three metal trailer steps, **with one gaffer-taped X on the bottom one**: a mark Take Two will never stand on.

- **CONTINUOUS ACTION:** ~155 frames, **six layers.**
  - **L1 TAKE TWO (f0 to f155).** **He never stops trying and he never gets anywhere.** A ~40-frame loop: shift weight forward, chair comes up an inch, shoulder meets the braced arm, settle back. **Four times.** By f120 he has stopped raising the chair and just stands, still committed, still there. **At f155 he is still in the doorway.**
  - **L2 TAKE ONE (f0 to f155).** The braced arm never moves. **He shifts his weight exactly once, at f96, and it is a bored shift, not a defensive one.** He is not fighting. He is leaning.
  - **L3 THE HERO (f0 to f155).** Packing. Never turns. **At f88 he pauses, half-turns his head 4px toward the trailer, and goes back to packing.** That is the closest anyone comes to the truth and it is the cruellest beat in the scene.
  - **L4 THE LIGHT (f0 to f155).** The trailer's door-spill flickers on a bad ballast, a slow irregular pulse. The chair's pool holds steady. Everything else is black.
  - **L5 HAZE (f0 to f155).** Drifting in both pools, never still.
  - **L6 CAMERA.** See camera.
  - **AT FRAME 155:** Take Two still committed in the doorway, Take One's arm still braced, the ballast still flickering, the hero still packing with his back to it, haze drifting, the better chair still held up in a doorway nobody is looking at. Cut from mid-motion.

- **Escalation:** **Escalates by making the audience want to shout.** f0 the deep composition lands and the frame does the work: better chair, blocked door, oblivious hero, all in one read. f0 to f88 Take Two tries four times, each attempt slightly less energetic, and the decay is the emotional line. **f88 the hero's 4px head-turn is the scene's peak and it goes nowhere.** f96 Take One shifts his weight out of boredom, which is more insulting than any effort would be. **f120 to f155 Take Two stops trying and just stands there holding it**, and the scene ends with a better answer simply existing, lit, twenty feet away, unwanted. **Nothing is resolved because nothing can be. That is the point.**

- **Gag / pop-culture:** **(1) THE BORED DOORMAN:** he blocks a door with one arm while looking somewhere else entirely. **(2) THE 4-PIXEL HEAD TURN**, and back. **(3) THE TAPED X ON THE BOTTOM STEP**, a mark for a take that never happens. **(4) THE BAD BALLAST**, flickering the only other light in the building.

- **Sprite costume:** **HERO:** foreground silhouette only, near-black, soft. **Eyes not needed, he is a back.** **TAKE ONE:** full bible spec, braced arm, bored. **TAKE TWO:** **identical slate mascot, identical silhouette, NO cap, NO board, NO stamp, eyes NOT visible (he is a take, not the hero).** He holds the better chair. **⛔ He never speaks, never gestures at the hero, never mugs at camera. He just tries to get to work.**

- **Camera:** **Key:** two pools only, the trailer's warm flickering door-spill and the chair's steady one, in a black-velvet void. **Height:** low, axis **y 620**, from behind the hero's ankles, deep focus, so foreground back / midground chair / far trailer all read at once. **Move:** a very slow continuous **3 percent push** the whole scene, threading between the hero's silhouette and the chair, **heading for the trailer door and never reaching it.** That unfinished push is the scene. Handheld ~1px.

- **Transition + SFX:** **IN:** hard cut on S0's last lamp clunk into near-black. **BEATS:** room tone is now huge and empty, a big black room. The **ballast buzz** is the bed. **Take Two's four attempts: a soft footfall and a fabric shift each time, and NO grunt, NO effort sound.** He is not straining, he is being calmly prevented. **The hero's toolbox: clinks, a lid, a latch.** f88 the packing stops for six frames and there is nothing but ballast buzz. **f96 Take One's weight shift: one bored boot scuff.** **⛔ Take One does not clap in this scene. He does not need to. Withholding it here makes S2's silence louder.** **OUT:** hard cut into S2 on a toolbox latch.

- **Mute check:** **PASS.** A stranger, sound off: **"There's another one of him in a doorway holding a much better chair, and the first one is leaning across the door so he can't get out, and the guy never looks round."** No header, no numeral.

- **Hook into next:** **The object carried is the better chair, unreachable.** **The question planted: what if he just asked for another go?** **S2 answers it and the answer is worse than not asking.**

### S2 THE DEFENCE

- **Takeaway:** Our guy finally asks for another take, and the grey copy doesn't re-shoot anything. He stands on top of the film can with his arms folded and hands him back the exact same reel.

- **Base object:** **THE REEL CAN, STOOD ON.** A flat circular film can with PRINT IT stamped on it, used as a plinth. Verb: **DEFEND**. Unique.

- **Story / metaphor:** **ASKING AGAIN MAKES IT WORSE.** This is the beat that kills the obvious objection ("why not just ask twice?") and it must land hard or the whole system beat is unearned. The hero finally does the reasonable thing: he turns around, gestures at the chair, asks for another go. **And Take One does not go back to work. He climbs on top of his own reel can, folds his arms, and holds the same reel out.** He is not being lazy. **He is defending.** He made that thing, so now he has something to protect, and every second he spends protecting it is a second he is not making a better one. **He is standing on his own work, literally.** The hero's nub is still out, still asking, and it stays out for the rest of the scene. **This is what "it remembers what it said and defends it instead of beating it" looks like as a physical event**, and it is why the fix has to be a fresh mind rather than a second question.

- **Set detail:** **Camera mid, axis y 500, straight on**, the most confrontational framing in the reel: two figures, one prop, no depth tricks.
  1. **THE REEL CAN.** **560, 620**, flat circle, **PRINT IT** stamped crooked on the lid. **He steps up onto it at f40** and it becomes a 60px plinth. The gag is that it is barely a plinth at all and he stands on it like a podium.
  2. **TAKE ONE, ARMS FOLDED.** **560, 460**, up on the can, **arms folded across the clapperboard**, holding it against his chest like a shield. **Cap still backward. Viewfinder still unused.** He holds the same reel out in one nub at f70 and **does not let go of it.**
  3. **THE SAME REEL.** A film reel, identical to the one already stamped, offered at **640, 500**. **⛔ It is visibly the SAME reel, not a new one.** Same scuffs, same tape flag.
  4. **THE HERO'S ASKING NUB.** **380, 500**, extended, open, **and it stays extended from f20 to f145.** It never gets filled with anything new. That held gesture is the scene's whole performance.
  5. **THE FINE CHAIR**, still on its mark at **300, 620**, still fine, now with a single lamp on it like an exhibit.
  6. **THE DEFENSIVE CLUTTER.** Around the can: **his own things, arranged.** A folded canvas chair-back, a coffee cup, a stack of two more cans. **He has moved in.** It reads as a man who has stopped shooting and started curating.
  7. **THE TRAILER**, far background, **door now shut**, one thin line of light at its edge. Take Two is still in there. The audience knows. The hero does not.
  8. **THE PRODUCTION BOARD**, rows 2 to 5 still blank. **Now lit by nothing.**
  9. **THE DARK BAYS**, present, unlit, patient.
  10. **THE HAZE**, one cone only, on the chair.

- **CONTINUOUS ACTION:** ~145 frames, **six layers.**
  - **L1 THE ASK (f0 to f145).** **f0 the hero is already turning**, mid-motion. f14 he gestures at the chair. **f20 his nub goes out and stays out.** f60, f96, f130: three small re-offers, each one smaller than the last, and the nub never comes down. **At f145 it is still out.**
  - **L2 THE CLIMB (f30 to f50).** f30 he notices the ask. f34 he looks at the can. **f40 he steps up onto it, unhurried.** f50 he settles.
  - **L3 THE FOLD (f50 to f145).** Arms fold at f56 across the board. **f70 the reel comes out.** f70 to f145 **he holds it exactly still.** He does not push it forward. He does not withdraw it. **He does not move again for 75 frames**, and the stillness is the performance.
  - **L4 THE CHAIR'S LAMP (f0 to f145).** A slow lazy sway on its stand, so the exhibit's shadow moves the whole scene and nothing in frame is ever fully static.
  - **L5 THE TRAILER LINE (f0 to f145).** The thin light at the shut door **flickers on the bad ballast**, far away, tiny, ignored.
  - **L6 CAMERA.** See camera.
  - **AT FRAME 145:** the hero's nub still out, Take One still folded on his can with the same reel held dead still, the exhibit lamp still swaying, the trailer's edge-light still flickering, haze drifting. **Nobody has moved for 75 frames and it is unbearable.** Cut from mid-motion.

- **Escalation:** **Escalates into stillness, which is the reel's boldest move and the reason S3 explodes.** f0 to f30 has motion: a turn, a gesture, an ask. **f40 the climb is the last real action in the scene.** f56 the fold. f70 the reel. **f70 to f145 is 75 frames of two figures not moving**, and it is the longest dead-still stretch in the reel by design. The audience's frustration builds with nothing to spend itself on. The three shrinking re-offers at f60, f96 and f130 are the only events and each one is smaller. **The scene does not end because something happens. It ends because the VO says "so stop asking once," and by then the audience is already screaming it.**

- **Gag / pop-culture:** **(1) STANDING ON HIS OWN WORK.** He is defending a chair by standing on a can, six inches off the ground, like a podium. **(2) THE SAME REEL, OFFERED AGAIN**, with the same scuffs. **(3) HE HAS MOVED IN:** a folded chair-back, a coffee cup, a stack of cans. He is not a filmmaker any more, he is a curator of one film. **(4) THE ARMS FOLDED ACROSS THE CLAPPERBOARD**, holding the thing that ends takes like a shield.

- **Sprite costume:** **HERO:** full spec, **eyes visible**, nub extended and held. Expression: reasonable, then patient, then **something dawning at f130.** He is not angry. He is realising. **TAKE ONE:** full bible spec, on the can, arms folded across the board, same reel offered, **absolutely motionless from f70.**

- **Camera:** **Key:** the swaying exhibit lamp on the chair, one hard rim on Take One's shoulder from a dead-ahead source, nothing else. Black-velvet void. **Height:** mid, axis **y 500**, **dead straight on**, symmetrical, confrontational. **Move:** locked f0 to f70 (matching the standoff), then **a slow 3 percent push into the held reel f70 to f145.** The camera gets closer to the same answer, which is the only progress anyone makes. **The push is the joke.**

- **Transition + SFX:** **IN:** hard cut on S1's toolbox latch. **BEATS:** f14 the hero's gesture, a fabric shift. **f40 the climb: one hollow metallic BONG as a boot lands on a film can**, and it is the funniest sound in the reel because it is so small. f56 arms fold, a leather-and-canvas creak. **f70 to f145 near-silence: the exhibit lamp's stand creaking on its sway, the distant ballast, room tone.** The three re-offers are almost inaudible fabric. **⛔ NO CLAP IN THIS SCENE. He does not need to end anything, he already did.** Withholding the clap for two straight scenes is what makes S3's five-clap failure land. **OUT:** hard cut into S3 **on the master switch's THUNK**, arriving early, over the last two frames of the standoff.

- **Mute check:** **PASS.** A stranger, sound off: **"He asks for another take and the grey one just stands on the film can with his arms folded and hands him back the same reel."** No header, no numeral.

- **Hook into next:** **The object carried is the hero's own dawning face at f130.** **The question planted: if asking twice doesn't work, what does?** S3 answers it with a switch.

### S3 FIVE STAGES

- **Takeaway:** He hits one big master switch and four more stages bang on at once, all identical, all with the same chair kit waiting, and the grey copy just stands there because he can't be in five places.

- **Base object:** **THE MASTER SWITCH.** One fat industrial knife-switch on the wall. Verb: **SWITCH ON**. Unique, and it is the reel's turn.

- **Story / metaphor:** **THE COMMAND, AS ONE PHYSICAL ACT.** The hero stops asking and does something instead. **He walks past Take One without looking at him** (the first time anyone has ignored him, and it costs Take One more than a fight would) and throws a master switch. **And the stages that were there the whole time bang on.** Four of them, identical, each with the same flat, the same mark, the same chalk, **the same chair kit stacked flat and waiting.** The audience has seen those bays in the dark since S0 f0 and this is the payoff: **nothing was built. Nothing was installed. They were always there.** That is the ignorance gap closing, staged as a light switch. **And Take One's problem arrives with the light: there is one of him, and there are five stages.** He looks left. He looks right. His whole authority was being the only one, and the room just made four more.

- **Set detail:** **Camera wide, axis y 440**, the widest frame since S0, looking straight down the row so all five bays are in one shot.
