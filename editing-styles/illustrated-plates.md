# Illustrated plates + authored 2D animation

**Status: optional alternative, not the default.** On September 21, 2026, Alex asked to preserve the SPLIT 161 technique as an alternative way to edit videos. Cinematic Blueprint remains the short-form default; the YouTube current direction is unchanged. This recipe documents a delivered approach, not a proven retention winner or blanket approval of every SPLIT revision.

## What the technique actually is

Generate painted **background plates**, then composite separately authored 2D actors and objects over them in Remotion. React/SVG and frame-based functions drive actions, expression, contact, lighting, camera and typography. This is layered 2D compositing with an illustrated sense of depth; it is not image-to-video generation or a simulated 3D scene.

In SPLIT, built-in image generation made two raster environments: a workshop and a drafting table. They remain mostly still. The original Claude sprite, OpenAI robot, hands, tools, power units, laptop, UI, code diff and real logos are independently rendered and animated. A generated engine cutout was tried and rejected; code-native geometry replaced it. Do not attribute all visible objects or the motion to image generation.

## Choose it when

Use when a narrated build, inspection or handoff benefits from a tactile illustrated environment and exact timed foreground performance. Choose it explicitly as the alternative style. Keep the standard approach when its existing sets work; prefer an actual screen capture when the viewer must see verified product behavior. This option does not convert fictional UI into product evidence.

## Reusable production recipe

1. Board the spoken verb and visible outcome before generating art. Name the dominant object, actor, action, reaction, consequence and receiving action. Establish audience identity immediately.
2. Generate the environment alone: no hero, characters, text, logos or UI baked into the plate. Reserve a quiet central acting area and header clearance. Specify camera angle, light direction, material palette and peripheral detail.
3. Inspect the actual crop. SPLIT requests were 4:3 plates, displayed with `objectFit: cover` in a 1012 × 792 panel. Prompt aspect ratio is not proof of final dimensions or safe placement. Keep grain, lamps and tools around the edges; remove competing hero-shaped scenery.
4. Composite the existing house characters and authentic logo assets. Use code-native props when identity, hinges, grips, independent moving parts or clear UI states matter. Generate a cutout only when its silhouette, alpha, scale and parts genuinely support the action.
5. Animate meaningful changes: Enter contact opens a second workspace; a code file is handed over, inspected and changed; a connector triggers sequential lights. Keep the background quiet when the foreground already explains the action. Do not add parallax just to claim that a still plate is animated.
6. Derive effect times from contacts. Keep voice at frame zero, maintain caption timing and review the music excerpt itself. Keep song-source offset distinct from where the music begins on the reel timeline.
7. Render the hook early. Inspect frame zero, 0–0.25s, 0.25–1s, the remaining hook and its handoff, then the whole reel. Review both native detail and phone-scale hierarchy. Fix faces, grips and readability before polishing.
8. Run the existing picture/audio/caption gates, then review the actual movie with audio. Preserve rejected drafts and disclose review gaps. Passing motion numbers do not validate the concept. Package matching source and verify the canonical Drive files.

## Prompt template

> A painted [environment] background plate for independently animated AI coding characters. [Camera/view], [consistent light], [materials and palette]. Keep the center [acting area] clear, with quiet space above for the house header and a foreground surface for contact. Put detailed [tools/scenery] around the perimeter. No characters, logos, typography, UI or central hero prop. This is scenery for compositing, not a completed animation frame.

The [exact three SPLIT prompts and asset outcomes](../memory/reels/evidence/split161/image-prompts.json) distinguish the two retained plates from the rejected engine. Prompt outputs require visual inspection; the words “transparent” and “empty” alone are not validation.

## Boundaries and failures to retain

- A detailed plate does not rescue generic rectangles, tiny logos, repetitive operations or idle characters.
- Real terminal/UI surfaces can be useful; decorative containers pretending to be explanations were rejected. Give them task-specific content and an actual state change.
- The ladybug/book hook was rejected as unrelated to the Claude audience. Pattern interruption must be immediately relevant, not merely surprising.
- Fast action does not require more cuts. Preserve one shared task and let its state evolve when the narration continues that task.
- Do not ease everything to rest before the next scene. Author a consequential exit or receiving movement; avoid jitter and stretched slow glides.
- For this optional recipe, the background plate may remain still. Assess purposeful foreground action and depth rather than forcing multiple animated background layers. This scoped choice does not replace other styles’ design requirements or waive the output checks.
- SPLIT’s later instruction excluded filmed B-roll. Its 8s music excerpt, shot count and exact caption/CTA times belong to this reel; do not silently universalize them.
