## Posting and checkpoints

This is a sequential organic experiment, not randomized audience assignment. Timing, audience overlap, repeat exposure and platform distribution can affect results. A shared body is intentional experimental control; image hashes do not predict whether a platform will distribute it similarly. Do not alter the body or colors to chase a supposed duplicate-detection score.

Suggested first block: choose three comparable posting slots in the normal schedule, spaced about48–72hours apart rather than dumping all three together. Use B→A→C on Instagram and C→B→A on YouTube as the initial planned orders; record actual order and timing. The opposite orders reduce one obvious ordering bias but do not remove confounding.

## Measure the hook separately from total reach

| Platform | Primary hook signal | Guardrails / secondary metrics |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | Studio “stayed to watch” versus swiped away, at matched post age | Retention at3/5/10s if available; average view duration and percentage viewed; engaged views; shares/comments/subscribers; raw views and Shorts-feed exposure as context. |

YouTube's average view duration for Shorts is based on engaged views, so it describes viewers who stayed. Read it alongside the swipe/stay signal, not as a substitute. Public views alone do not isolate hook effectiveness. [YouTube engagement definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9313698?hl=en), [Shorts analytics guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en-GB).

Never pool raw Instagram and YouTube views or rates into a single score. Their definitions and audiences differ. Compare each variant against A within the same platform, topic and checkpoint. Record the exact dashboard metric label/denominator. Leave unavailable fields blank, not zero. Do not compute “stayed to watch” as engaged views divided by all views.

## Long-term decision rule, proposed before results

JEV is block1, a pilot. Repeat these three families on at least six matched topics before making one the default. Keep the same body within each topic; vary the order across topics using the six orders ABC, ACB, BAC, BCA, CAB, CBA. The same topic never proves a generally winning family.

Use1,000 relevant opportunities per variant as a practical minimum for considering a block, not a power calculation or a guarantee: Shorts-feed opportunities where supplied on YouTube; eligible initial plays for the Instagram hold metric where supplied. If only reach is available, label it a proxy and do not infer a missing denominator. Too little comparable exposure means inconclusive, not a loss.

Proposed promotion rule per platform: a challenger beats A on the chosen primary metric in at least4of6 eligible matched topics and has median lift of at least3percentage points, with no consistent deterioration in5–10s retention or average watch time. These are working decision thresholds, not claims of statistical significance. If using average watch time as the Instagram fallback, preregister a relative-lift threshold (suggest10%) instead of mixing seconds with percentage points. Do not switch the primary metric after seeing which one wins.

Inspect where the curve drops. A lift before3.6s followed by a bigger drop immediately after the payoff suggests the hook attracts attention but sets the wrong expectation. A weak opener with strong later retention suggests recognition or first motion needs work. In either case change the mechanism/timing for the next experiment and give it a new variant ID; don't silently edit the file behind existing results.

After a repeatable winner, allocate roughly70% of future eligible posts to that family,20% to the next strongest,10% to a new challenger. This is a suggested exploration policy, not a platform rule. Keep separate winners for Instagram and YouTube if the evidence differs. Test payoff timing or sound as a new experiment only after family choice; do not simultaneously change topic, voice, body, timing and soundtrack.
