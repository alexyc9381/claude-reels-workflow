- Never tell viewers in the video that they must join, sign up or start a free trial to obtain the resource. No trial wording on badges, cards, footers or closing graphics. This changes video communication, not website access rules or required checkout disclosures; do not silently alter the existing membership funnel.

## Voice, content and source handling

Remotion, landscape 16:9. Identify the actual master narration source (external voiceover or OBS); mute camera scratch audio after sync. Do not assume OBS exists. Measure offset/drift and preserve original speech speed. One cumulative frame edit map governs video, audio, animations, SFX and chapters.

Preserve complete useful teaching and natural script deviations. Audit discarded material against the whole recording and supplied script. Remove dead air, failed takes, duplicate attempts and spoken editing directions (e.g. “cut cut”). A look-away during useful speech calls for a picture cutaway, not deleting the explanation. Never pad a six-minute lesson to eight minutes with duplicate speech, slow playback or silent demos already covered during narration.

Protect whole words and natural breath handles; inspect both sides of every cut. Tighten awkward inter-section pauses without creating clipped word tails. Filenames, transcripts and silence detection are clues, not sync or cut proof. Report when camera files are actually copied/verified before saying the camera can be unplugged. Check space first; do not promise a 31GB copy on a 5GB disk. Upload only when authorized; the latest request authorizes the finished V7 file, not raw footage.

## Visual language

Use the warm Claude tutorial system: cream/paper #F4F1EA, ink #292822, clay #C97355, warm peach accents; restrained borders, shadows and rounded surfaces. Large editorial Georgia-style headings and simple Arial-style interface/body type are valid in this system, superseding the previous blanket serif ban and mandatory white-glass/Manrope treatment. Adapt to content, keep contrast and avoid generic toy-like machinery or template-looking folder blocks.

Prominent original orange 2D Claude sprites participate throughout: look, inspect, operate, drag, hand off, celebrate. Distinguish roles through outfits and jobs. Model comparison reference: Haiku bare; Sonnet shirt; Opus suit/tie/glasses. Reuse rigs and helpers, never blindly replay whole scenes. Use authentic recognizable logos in original brand colors (including genuinely monochrome marks).

Every scene has a beginning → meaningful action → visible result aligned with the spoken explanation. “Write the email” shows writing and a finished draft; “hundreds of connectors” shows varied recognizable app marks and a workflow; a Skill shows reusable instructions driving steps/output. One idea per scene; no title/subtitle/footer paraphrasing the same message three times. Use plain beginner language, not unnecessarily technical document labels.

Detailed means explanatory detail, not clutter. A rich Artifacts demo can build code, update a preview, show a chart/tooltip and publish. A simple model-choice explanation should stay simple. The second half needs the same purposeful animation and sound attention as the intro. No long static holds, decorative wiggles in place of actions, generic squares or repeated app diagrams.

## Timing, cursor and layout

- Opening must pass the small-player/squint test: large subjects, high contrast, immediate readable action and varied sound. Hold presenter full-screen roughly two seconds before splitting; do not make the face uncomfortably tight. Keep bed/background distractions out through the transition. A subtle simulated dolly zoom is an available treatment, not mandatory for every video. Inspect mattes/edges and preserve headroom.
- Alternate camera side between suitable modules; move full panels, never mirror faces/logos/text. Preserve shared coordinate systems for pointers and overlays.
- Mouse arrives before activation; click/hover highlight and pointer share a clock. During dragging keep pointer offset fixed to the dragged object. Use circles, temporary zooms and animated perimeter traces to focus attention at the spoken mention.
- Persistent readable top-right section badge helps scrubbing. Full-screen chapter cards explicitly say MODULE 1, MODULE 2, etc. Large readable module text; avoid duplicate section labels at the left. Brief transitions before/after; no narration-free padding.
- Before the next section, an orange line traces the webcam perimeter as anticipation. UI borders can trace on meaningful reveals, not constantly compete with narration.

## Audio

Narration leads. Purposeful SFX throughout the entire timeline: click, typing, drag/contact, slider movement, source/file arrival, reveal, completion and handoff. Match cues to actual action events, not generic fixed intervals. Use varied premium-feeling families; avoid repeating pencil brushing or dings for everything. Dings can punctuate model highlights, not every component. Stronger layered first 3–5 seconds, but intelligible voice and controlled peaks.

Quiet tutorial-style restrained synth/percussion after the intro; the piano choice was rejected. Do not hardcode this project's song or gains as universal defaults. Check mixed listening and stems; audibility/intelligibility cannot be proven from cue counts alone. Keep licensing provenance and necessary credit in the description, never a persistent video footer. Do not call attribution-required music copyright-free.

Do not rebuild approved sections unnecessarily; unchanged picture chunks can be reused only when inputs match. One continuous final audio mix prevents chunk AAC gaps. Never overwrite hardlinked prior renders.

Technical passes are not creative approval or evidence of retention improvement.
