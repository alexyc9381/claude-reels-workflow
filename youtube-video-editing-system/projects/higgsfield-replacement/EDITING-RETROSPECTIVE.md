## What the revision process taught us

| Repeated problem | Durable decision and why | Review gate |
|---|---|---|
| Animated text and rectangles did not explain the speech | Extract the spoken noun, verb, quantity and order. Stage a recognizable beginning → action → changed result. Simple concept, detailed mechanics. | Watch without audio: can someone understand what changed? |
| More motion became clutter | Give one large action priority; overlap related supporting jobs. Additional props must explain something. No arbitrary forest, floating labels or ornament. | Remove any object with no explanatory role; inspect peak overlap. |
| Sprites were passive or stopped abruptly | Use the original short-form 2D rig, role outfits, symmetric eyes, anticipation, physical contact and damped recovery. Receivers react; carried objects follow the carrier. | Review approach, contact and settlement, not just a still frame. |
| Repeated scenes felt generic | Reuse rigs/materials, not full choreography. Give each spoken idea a distinct mechanism and payoff. | Compare the scene map across the whole film. |
| Intro lacked a clear promise | Large playing comparison clips, readable 1/2 identities, presenter large-to-inset, distinct countdown, then recognizable Higgsfield/Claude rivalry and workflow. | Check first 1/3/5/10/30 seconds at small size and without audio. Final title is still needed for exact packaging alignment. |
| Orange hid Claude; prices and cards overlapped | Pale warm actor fields, stronger orange edges, silhouette separation, white glass and dark Manrope text. Center price/unit as one bounded group. | Check actual encoded frames at phone-preview size. |
| Graphics obscured teaching or showed the wrong result | Keep OBS large, crop menu/Dock, protect typing fields. Zoom the actual synchronized source; do not substitute another video in a review card. | Check each active input and the matching result frame. |
| Crops and blur hurt the presenter | Separate intro and tutorial crops; blur the room through a person matte, never the face. Inset enlargement is geometry, not tighter cropping. | Check opening, transitions and tutorial poses independently. |
| Overcutting removed useful material or word tails | OBS-only narration, clap-measured Sony sync, source-led content preservation and complete phonemes. Distinguish retakes/dead time from useful explanation and generated playback. | Listen across every changed join; check marker words, duplicates and final word. |
| Static privacy overlay did not teach the task | Opaque secret protection plus one sequential synthetic create/copy/store demonstration, with a clear privacy explanation. | Check coverage through transitions; never put real keys into graphics or Git. |
| Sound was absent, repetitive or too loud | Quiet tactile cues tied to actions, restrained finite music, ducking for VO and reactions. Countdown predicts the actual edit event. | Listen to the encoded mix; meters alone do not establish quality. |
| Hook-only previews missed whole-film requests | Keep a request-to-source map and explicit scope. Deliver a complete export when requested, not merely an intro proof. | Verify source hash, full decode, chapters, sample frames and review limits. |

## Latest choices to preserve

- Remotion, not Supereditor. Separate this system from Matchtern and the short-form output chassis while borrowing the short-form acting principles.
- Original 2D Claude remains; the CGI pet was explicitly removed from this film. Preserve its provenance for future separately authorized use.
- Real company/model marks aid recognition; an editorial Higgsfield mascot is not its official logo. Logo presence does not establish universal tool compatibility.
- “Hundreds” needs visible accumulation; multi-step instructions need ordered action. V23 preserves V22's positively received direction rather than resetting the visual system.
- Large A/B evidence gets fewer overlays. Do not reveal brand identities prematurely. Apply comparison treatments consistently; editorial sound must not be represented as generated model audio.
- Price units stay honest: a monthly subscription and a per-generation example are different measures. Neither the thumbnail nor an animation verifies equal-settings savings.
- Simple description chapters use “Why…” and “Step 1…” labels. [Pasteable chapters](youtube-chapters.txt) were updated after the MP4; embedded titles and the older bundle were not silently changed.

## Handoff and publication lessons

Git stores code, decisions, manifests and verification—not raw recordings, credentials or full exports. An editable handoff needs working media, source history, launch instructions and checksum verification; an MP4 alone is insufficient. [V23 Mac handoff](OTHER-MAC-HANDOFF-V23.md) records the package and limits. The other physical Mac has not been tested. A local Drive copy or login is not proof of upload; no successful Drive upload was verified. A Downloads copy was supplied for manual transfer.

For publication, put the community link first in the description, then the actual tutorial promise, free skill/setup and real camera-direction bonus, simple chapters and required music attribution. Free setup does not mean free generation. Unfilled public links remain placeholders. Promotional copy should be direct and confident; Alex rejected the tearful launch emoji. A still-only Story sequence can use a personal launch, thumbnail comparison premise and resource CTA; these are proposed copy choices, not measured conversion results.

## Before declaring the next revision done

1. Map each request to a source segment and current time; feedback timestamps belong to the reviewed version.
2. Record what changed, what stayed intact and what remains unverified.
3. Review action/payoff frames, collisions, small-preview legibility and sensitive-screen seams.
4. Check changed audio joins and full playback; keep machine checks distinct from human listening and creative approval.
5. Retiming must propagate to graphics, subtitles if present, countdowns and chapters.
6. Update the learning index and canonical memory, commit only scoped files, push and verify remote HEAD. A documentation push is not a new render or publication.

No retention analytics have been supplied. Retention improvements above are design hypotheses and user preferences, not demonstrated uplift.
